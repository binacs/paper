# Analysis of Large-Scale Multi-Tenant GPU Clusters for DNN Training Workloads

## TL;DR

This paper presents a detailed workload characterization of a two-month trace from a large enterprise's multi-tenant GPU cluster used for DNN training, identifying key factors that affect cluster utilization and providing design guidelines for next-generation schedulers.

## Problem & Motivation

Large enterprises increasingly train DNN models on shared, multi-tenant GPU clusters. Existing scheduling frameworks aim to provide high efficiency, resource isolation, and fair sharing, but DNN workloads differ from traditional big data analytics in two significant ways: GPUs are monolithic resources that cannot be finely shared, and deep learning frameworks require gang scheduling, reducing scheduling flexibility and making jobs inelastic to failures. These differences can lead to poor cluster utilization if not properly addressed, yet there is limited understanding of how these characteristics impact real-world cluster performance.

## Key Ideas

- Analyze a two-month trace from a production multi-tenant GPU cluster, correlating scheduler logs with per-job logs.
- Identify three distinct issues affecting cluster utilization: (1) impact of gang scheduling and locality constraints on queuing, (2) impact of locality on GPU utilization, and (3) failures during training.
- Provide design guidelines for next-generation cluster schedulers based on operational experience.

## System Design

The paper does not propose a new system but analyzes an existing production cluster. The methodology involves collecting and correlating scheduler logs and job-level logs from a multi-tenant GPU cluster over two months. The analysis focuses on three areas: queuing behavior under gang scheduling and locality constraints, GPU utilization patterns related to locality, and failure characteristics during training.

## Evaluation

The paper validates its claims through a detailed workload characterization of a two-month trace from a large enterprise's multi-tenant GPU cluster. It does not compare against baselines but provides empirical evidence of the issues and derives design guidelines from the analysis.

## Takeaways

- Gang scheduling and locality constraints can cause significant queuing delays, reducing cluster utilization.
- Locality (e.g., GPU placement relative to data or other GPUs) affects GPU utilization, potentially leading to underutilization.
- Failures during training are common and can impact job completion times, requiring fault-tolerance mechanisms.
- Next-generation schedulers should consider these DNN-specific characteristics to improve efficiency.

## Related Work

The paper positions itself against traditional cluster computing workloads and scheduling frameworks, highlighting the differences in resource granularity and scheduling flexibility. It does not directly compare with specific systems but provides a characterization that can inform future scheduler designs.

## Open Questions

- The paper does not propose specific scheduling algorithms; it leaves the implementation of design guidelines as future work.
- The analysis is based on a single cluster trace; generalizability to other environments is unclear.
- The paper does not quantify the potential improvement from following the design guidelines; further research could evaluate proposed changes.

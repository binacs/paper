# Analysis of Large-Scale Multi-Tenant GPU Clusters for DNN Training Workloads

## TL;DR

Presents a two-month workload characterization of a large enterprise's multi-tenant GPU cluster for DNN training, correlating scheduler logs with job logs to study how gang scheduling, locality constraints, and training failures affect utilization, and derives design guidelines for next-generation cluster schedulers.

## Problem & Motivation

Large enterprises increasingly train ML models on shared, multi-tenant GPU clusters, and schedulers aim for efficiency, isolation, and fair sharing as in traditional cluster computing. However, DNN training workloads differ in two key ways: GPUs are monolithic resources that cannot be shared at fine granularity across users, and deep learning frameworks require gang scheduling, which reduces scheduling flexibility and makes jobs inelastic to runtime failures.

The paper argues that these differences are not well understood, so it characterizes a real production trace to identify what actually limits cluster utilization for DNN training on multi-tenant clusters.

## Key Ideas

- Detailed workload characterization of a two-month trace from a multi-tenant GPU cluster in a large enterprise.
- Correlation of scheduler logs with per-job logs to connect scheduling decisions to job-level behavior.
- Three distinct issues affecting cluster utilization are studied: (1) the effect of gang scheduling and locality constraints on queuing, (2) the effect of locality on GPU utilization, and (3) failures during training.
- Design guidelines for next-generation cluster schedulers for DNN training workloads, grounded in large-scale operational experience.

## System Design

The work is an empirical measurement study rather than a new system. Its methodology combines cluster-level scheduler logs with logs emitted by individual training jobs, joined to reconstruct how scheduling constraints and job behavior interact over a two-month period in a production multi-tenant GPU cluster.

## Evaluation

Validation is via analysis of a two-month production trace from a large enterprise's multi-tenant GPU cluster. The study examines queuing effects from gang scheduling and locality constraints, GPU utilization as affected by locality, and training failures, using correlated scheduler and job logs. The abstract does not report specific headline numbers.

## Takeaways

- GPU monolithicity and gang scheduling are first-order constraints on multi-tenant DNN training clusters, distinct from traditional big data analytics assumptions.
- Locality constraints can hurt both queuing and GPU utilization, so placement policy matters for efficiency.
- Training failures are a significant operational factor and must be accounted for in scheduler design.
- Correlating scheduler logs with job logs is a practical methodology for understanding utilization in production ML clusters.
- Scheduler design for DNN training should be informed by production workload characterization rather than assumptions carried over from big data workloads.

## Related Work

The abstract positions the work against existing cluster computing scheduling frameworks that target high efficiency, resource isolation, and fair sharing for traditional big data analytics workloads, arguing DNN training workloads differ in GPU granularity and gang scheduling requirements.

- How the findings generalize beyond this single enterprise's two-month trace and cluster configuration is unclear.

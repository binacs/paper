# Analysis of Large-Scale Multi-Tenant GPU Clusters for DNN Training Workloads

## TL;DR

This paper presents a detailed workload characterization of a two-month trace from a multi-tenant GPU cluster used for DNN training, identifying key differences from traditional big data workloads and providing design guidelines for next-generation cluster schedulers.

## Problem & Motivation

Large enterprises are increasingly training DNN models on shared, multi-tenant GPU clusters. Unlike traditional big data analytics workloads, DNN training workloads present unique challenges: GPUs are monolithic resources that cannot be shared at fine granularity, and deep learning frameworks require gang scheduling, which reduces scheduling flexibility and makes jobs inelastic to failures. These differences affect cluster utilization and scheduling efficiency, but there is a lack of empirical understanding of real-world multi-tenant GPU cluster workloads.

## Key Ideas

- GPUs are monolithic resources, limiting fine-grained sharing across users.
- Gang scheduling is required for DNN training, reducing scheduling flexibility and making jobs inelastic to failures.
- Workload characterization from a two-month trace of a multi-tenant GPU cluster in a large enterprise.
- Correlation of scheduler logs with individual job logs to study three issues: (1) effect of gang scheduling and locality constraints on queuing, (2) effect of locality on GPU utilization, and (3) failures during training.
- Design guidelines for next-generation cluster schedulers for DNN training workloads.

## Evaluation

The paper validates its claims through a detailed workload characterization of a two-month long trace from a multi-tenant GPU cluster in a large enterprise. It correlates scheduler logs with individual job logs to analyze three distinct issues affecting cluster utilization: queuing due to gang scheduling and locality constraints, GPU utilization affected by locality, and failures during training. The abstract does not provide specific headline numbers.

## Takeaways

- DNN training workloads on multi-tenant GPU clusters differ significantly from traditional big data workloads due to monolithic GPUs and gang scheduling requirements.
- Gang scheduling and locality constraints can lead to increased queuing and reduced cluster utilization.
- Locality impacts GPU utilization, and failures during training are a significant concern.
- Design guidelines for next-generation schedulers are derived from operational experience.

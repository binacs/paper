# AWQ: Activation-aware Weight Quantization for LLM Compression and Acceleration

> **Why it matters:** AWQ made 4-bit weight-only (W4A16) LLM serving a practical default by protecting the ~1% of activation-salient weight channels through an equivalent per-channel scaling, rather than mixed precision or reconstruction. Its co-designed TinyChat kernels showed that the gain comes from cutting weight memory traffic in memory-bound decoding.

(Author list truncated to 8; the paper also lists Chuang Gan and Song Han. The MLSys proceedings and the current arXiv HTML render the title as "...for On-Device LLM Compression and Acceleration"; the arXiv abstract page uses the title above.)

## Problem

At batch size 1, LLM token generation is dominated by reading weights from DRAM, not by compute. Weights cost too much memory for edge devices and single GPUs. In FP16, GPT-3 (175B) needs 350 GB (Sec. 1). Quantization-aware training is too expensive at LLM scale. Low-bit post-training quantization either loses accuracy (round-to-nearest, RTN) or uses second-order reconstruction (GPTQ), which can overfit the calibration set and hurt generalization to other domains, instruction-tuned models and multimodal models (Sec. 1–2). Mixed-precision schemes that keep outliers in FP16 recover accuracy, but they are hardware-unfriendly.

## Core design

### Observation: a small set of salient weights, found through activations (Sec. 3.1)
- Keeping just 0.1–1% of weight *input channels* in FP16 (rest INT3, group size 128) recovers most of the perplexity lost to RTN. This only works when the channels are chosen by **activation magnitude**. Choosing by weight magnitude or at random barely helps (Table 1).
- Intuition: input channels that see large activations contribute most to the output, so their quantization error matters most.

### Protecting salient channels by scaling, not mixed precision (Sec. 3.2)
- Quantizer: Δ = max(|w|)/2^(N−1), Q(w) = Δ·Round(w/Δ) (Eq. 1). Multiplying a weight by s > 1 and dividing its input activation by s leaves the math unchanged. The scale Δ′ of the group barely moves, so the relative error on that channel shrinks by about 1/s (Eq. 2–3).
- Too large an s inflates Δ′ for the whole group and hurts the non-salient channels. On OPT-6.7B, s = 2 is the sweet spot and s = 4 is worse (Table 2).
- **Search:** the objective minimizes the output difference of each linear layer after quantization (Eq. 4). The scale is parameterized as s = s_X^α, where s_X is the average per-channel activation magnitude from calibration data. α is grid-searched over [0, 1] with 20 points (Eq. 5). Weight clipping is then tuned to minimize MSE.
- No backpropagation and no weight reconstruction. Calibration only collects activation statistics, which is why the method is claimed to be robust to calibration-set distribution.
- Deployment format: weight-only, grouped quantization (group size 128), INT4/INT3. The calibration set is drawn from the Pile (Sec. 3.2 / 5.1). The scales fold into the preceding operator or into the weights, so inference sees plain uniform low-bit weights.

### TinyChat: the inference system (Sec. 4)
- **Why W4A16 (Sec. 4.1, Fig. 3):** on an RTX 4090 with LLaMA-7B, generation dominates latency over prefill. FP16 decoding has an arithmetic intensity of about 1 FLOP/byte, far below the 4090's ridge point. Weight access dominates memory traffic, so 4-bit weights raise attainable throughput roughly in proportion to the bit reduction.
- **On-the-fly dequantization:** INT4 weights are dequantized to FP16 inside the GEMM/GEMV kernel and never written back to DRAM.
- **SIMD-aware weight packing (Fig. 4):** on ARM NEON, 32 4-bit weights are interleaved in a 128-bit register (w0, w16, w1, w17, …) so that three SIMD instructions unpack all 32. Conventional packing needs about three scalar instructions per weight. On GPUs, each group of 8 weights is packed in the order {0,2,4,6,1,3,5,7}.
- **Kernel fusion:** LayerNorm ops are fused into one kernel. The QKV projections are fused, rotary/positional embedding is computed on the fly, and preallocated KV caches are updated inside the attention kernel. Per-kernel FP16 compute on the 4090 is comparable to kernel-launch overhead, so cutting the number of launches pays off directly. This matters most for models with inefficient forward passes, such as Falcon and StarCoder.
- Backends: CUDA/PTX, NEON and AVX with a PyTorch frontend. On GPUs only attention, LayerNorm and linear kernels are custom. On CPUs the whole graph is lowered to C++.

## Limitations

- **Weight-only.** Activations and the KV cache stay FP16. Compute goes through FP16 tensor cores after dequantization. This is ideal for batch-1, memory-bound decoding. (Own analysis) At large serving batch sizes, where GEMMs become compute-bound, dequantization overhead erodes the gain. This motivated later W4A8/KV4 work (e.g., QServe from the same group).
- **Heuristic scale family.** s = s_X^α with one α per layer is a 1-D search. It does not jointly optimize weights the way GPTQ does, and at INT2 AWQ alone is not enough: the paper pairs it with GPTQ (Table 9).
- **Evaluation leans on perplexity** for most model families, with smaller task suites for instruction-tuned, code and math models. Vicuna is judged by GPT-4 pairwise comparison (Fig. 5).
- **Calibration robustness is shown on narrow cases** (PubMed/Enron cross-domain, OPT-6.7B; Fig. 8b), not across many domains.
- **Inconsistencies noticed in the paper (current arXiv HTML):**
  - The text says "We provide an ablation study on OPT models under INT3-g128 quantization in Table 5", but Table 5 holds Mistral-7B/Mixtral-8x7B results. Table 5 also reports a *higher* FP16 WikiText-2 PPL for Mixtral-8x7B (5.94) than for Mistral-7B (4.14). That is surprising and may be a column swap, so its numbers are omitted from Key numbers.
  - The intro gives about 30 tokens/s for 13B on an RTX 4070, while Sec. 5.4 gives 33 tokens/s.
  - The Table 7 (VILA) caption calls INT4 "lossless", yet some benchmarks drop (e.g., VILA-7B POPE 86.3 → 85.3).
  - The OPT-1.3B INT3 RTN PPL is 119.00 in Table 1 but 119.47 in Table 3.
  - Table 4's model-family headers and parts of Tables 8–9 extract garbled, so Table 4 is left out entirely and only unambiguous rows of Tables 8–9 are used. The Fig. 10 speedups over other systems and the Raspberry Pi figure are also left out, because they could not be checked against the paper text here.

## Relation to other systems

- **GPTQ:** the main baseline. GPTQ uses layer-wise second-order reconstruction that updates the remaining weights to compensate error. AWQ uses no reconstruction, only activation-guided scaling plus clipping, and needs fewer calibration samples (Fig. 8a). The two are complementary: AWQ+GPTQ is used at INT2 (Table 9).
- **LLM.int8() / mixed-precision outlier schemes (SpQR-style):** these keep outlier features or weights in higher precision with irregular storage. AWQ gets a similar protective effect from an *equivalent transform*, so storage stays uniform INT4 and kernel-friendly.
- **SmoothQuant (same lab):** also uses per-channel scaling, but to migrate activation outliers into weights for W8A8. AWQ scales salient weight channels *up* to shrink their relative weight-quantization error, in a W4A16 regime.
- **llama.cpp / exllama / AutoGPTQ / FasterTransformer:** these are the systems baselines. TinyChat is compared against them on Jetson Orin in Fig. 10. (Own analysis) Its distinguishing pieces are fused dequant-in-GEMV kernels, SIMD-aware packing and aggressive fusion of small ops to fight launch overhead.
- **vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, HF TGI, LMDeploy:** these adopted AWQ checkpoints and kernels as a W4A16 format. The project page and README list these integrations; they are not evaluated in the paper.

## Takeaways for practice

- In batch-1 or low-batch decoding, profile bytes moved, not FLOPs. Cutting weights to 4 bits roughly scales attainable throughput by the bit reduction when weight traffic dominates (Fig. 3).
- Use activation statistics, not weight magnitudes, to decide what to protect. The same principle applies to choosing which layers or channels get higher precision in any compression pipeline.
- Prefer equivalent transforms (scaling folded into adjacent ops) over irregular mixed precision. Uniform layouts keep kernels simple and fast.
- Fuse dequantization into the matmul. Never materialize FP16 weights in DRAM.
- For small-kernel decoding on fast GPUs, kernel-launch overhead is first-order. Fuse LayerNorm, QKV and KV-cache updates, or use graph capture.
- (Own analysis) For a serving platform: W4A16 AWQ wins for latency-bound or memory-capacity-bound deployments, such as edge devices, single-GPU large models and low-QPS endpoints. For high-batch throughput serving, benchmark against FP8 or W4A8 paths, since dequantization becomes compute overhead there. Treat the quantization format as a per-workload scheduling decision.
- A small calibration set suffices (16 sequences, Fig. 8a), so quantization can be a routine step in a model-release pipeline.

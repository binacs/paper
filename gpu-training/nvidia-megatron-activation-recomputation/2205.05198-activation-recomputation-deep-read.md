# Reducing Activation Recomputation in Large Transformer Models

> **Why it matters:** It gives a closed-form per-layer activation-memory model and two cheap techniques (sequence parallelism and selective recomputation) that let 175B–1T models train without full activation checkpointing, recovering about 30% throughput; both are now defaults in Megatron-style stacks.

## Problem

At hundreds of billions of parameters, tensor parallelism (TP) and pipeline parallelism (PP) make the weights fit, but activations do not. TP only splits activations inside the attention and MLP GEMMs, and PP does not reduce the activation memory held by the first stage. The usual fix, full activation recomputation (checkpointing at layer boundaries), costs 30–40% extra execution time (§1). The paper asks how much of that recomputation is actually necessary.

## Core design

### Activation-memory model (§4)

Notation: s sequence length, b microbatch size, h hidden size, a attention heads, t TP size, p PP size, L layers. Activations are 16-bit and dropout masks are 1 byte per element (§4.1). Per layer:

- Attention block: 11sbh + 5as²b. This is the QKV input, Q and K, V, the projection input and a dropout mask (11sbh), plus the softmax output, its dropout mask and the dropout output (5as²b).
- MLP: 19sbh. LayerNorms: 4sbh.
- Total, no parallelism (Eq. 1): **sbh(34 + 5as/h)**.
- With TP (Eq. 2): **sbh(10 + 24/t + 5as/(ht))**. The 10sbh term is not divided by t because the LayerNorms, the post-block dropouts and the block inputs are replicated across the TP group (§4.2.1).
- With TP + sequence parallelism (Eq. 4): **sbh/t · (34 + 5as/h)**, so everything is divided by t.
- Under the 1F1B schedule the first stage holds p in-flight microbatches × L/p layers, so total memory is (sbhL/t)(34 + 5as/h) regardless of p (Eq. 5). The interleaved schedule multiplies this by (1 + (p−1)/(pm)) (§4.2.3).
- Embedding, final-LN and output-layer terms are under 0.01% for a 22B model and are ignored (§4.3).

### Sequence parallelism (§4.2.2)

LayerNorm and dropout are independent along the sequence dimension, so the regions that TP leaves replicated are split along s instead. Two conjugate operators replace Megatron's f/f̄ all-reduce pair:

- **g**: all-gather along s in the forward pass and reduce-scatter in the backward pass. It sits before the column-parallel GEMM, where the full sequence is needed.
- **ḡ**: reduce-scatter along s in the forward pass and all-gather in the backward pass. It sits after the row-parallel GEMM, so the following dropout and LayerNorm see an s/t shard (Eq. 3, Figs. 5–6).

A ring all-reduce is already a reduce-scatter followed by an all-gather, so SP+TP moves the same bytes as TP alone. The paper's claim is that SP adds no communication bandwidth. It only re-partitions the non-GEMM region between the two halves of what used to be the all-reduce. SP lives inside the TP group, so it inherits TP's intra-node (NVLink) placement.

### Selective activation recomputation (§5)

The 5as/h term comes entirely from the attention core: QKᵀ, softmax, softmax dropout and attention over V. That part has large activations but few FLOPs. The paper checkpoints everything except this region (Fig. 3, red box) and recomputes only it in the backward pass. Total memory becomes 34sbhL/t (Eq. 6).

- GPT-3 175B (a=96, s=2048, h=12288): 5as/h = 80, so the technique saves about 70% of activation memory for about 2.7% extra FLOPs.
- MT-NLG 530B (a=128, h=20480): 5as/h = 64, giving about 65% saved for about 1.6% extra FLOPs.
- It pays off most when 5as/h > 34.

### Pipeline-specific extras (Appendices B, C, arXiv v1)

- **Output-tensor deallocation:** each microbatch's stage-output tensor is freed after its forward pass instead of being held until the backward pass. This saves about 2.73 GB on the first stage of the 530B model (App. B), and all §6 results use it.
- **Microbatch-level recomputation:** later pipeline stages hold fewer in-flight microbatches, so they can skip recomputation for some of them. The paper calls the MFU gain "very modest" (App. C).

## Limitations

- **First-stage and non-uniform memory (paper).** The first pipeline stage still holds a full L-layer worth of activations, and memory is uneven across stages. The paper lists these, plus fragmentation with large microbatches, as future work (§7).
- **Gradient all-reduce not overlapped (paper).** The DP experiment does not overlap the data-parallel gradient all-reduce, so the 56.0% → 54.2% MFU drop is partly an artifact. The paper argues overlap would remove most of it (§6.3).
- **SP is capped by the TP group (own analysis).** SP shards only across the TP ranks, typically 8 within an NVLink domain. It does not help when s grows faster than t can, and it does not split the attention core's s² term along the sequence. Long-context training later needed separate context-parallel or ring-attention schemes.
- **Selective recompute is largely subsumed by fused attention (own analysis).** The recomputed region is exactly what FlashAttention never materializes. With a fused attention kernel the 5as²b term disappears by construction, and the remaining question is the 34sbh/t term. The paper predates FlashAttention's integration into Megatron.
- **Narrow evaluation (own analysis).** All runs use one cluster, A100s, s = 2048, a fixed t = 8 and dense GPT models. There is no evaluation with MoE, GQA/MQA, other hardware or other sequence lengths. Weight and optimizer memory are out of scope.
- **Version drift (own observation).** The HFU values differ between the arXiv and MLSys versions. In the MLSys version, Table 5 lists 56.5% HFU for 1T while §6.3 says 57.0%. The App. C 175B figure (52.3%, +0.7) does not add up from Table 5's 51.4%.

## Relation to other systems

- **Megatron-LM PTD-P (Narayanan et al., SC 2021).** Its abstract combines tensor, pipeline and data parallelism and proposes an interleaved schedule worth more than 10% throughput, training 1T parameters on 3,072 GPUs at 502 PFLOP/s (52% of peak). This paper keeps that 3D layout and its interleaved schedule and attacks what PTD-P left on the table: activation memory, which PTD-P still handled with full recomputation. The f/f̄ all-reduces become g/ḡ.
- **ZeRO (Rajbhandari et al., SC 2020).** Its abstract targets memory redundancy in data- and model-parallel training, training over 100B parameters on 400 GPUs at about 15 PFLOP/s. ZeRO shards model state across DP ranks, while this paper shards activations across TP ranks. The paper calls ZeRO and offloading complementary but less efficient at large GPU counts (§2).
- **PyTorch FSDP (Zhao et al., VLDB 2023).** Its abstract describes an industry-grade fully sharded data-parallel system co-designed with PyTorch's tensor, dispatcher and caching allocator, with performance comparable to DDP and near-linear TFLOPS scaling. Like ZeRO it shards model state, not activations. FSDP users still rely on activation checkpointing, where the selective-recompute idea applies directly.
- **FlashAttention (Dao et al., NeurIPS 2022).** Its abstract presents an exact attention kernel that tiles to cut HBM↔SRAM reads and writes, reporting a 3× speedup on GPT-2 (1K). It removes the same s²-sized tensors that selective recomputation targets, but at the kernel level: it recomputes attention blockwise in the backward pass from stored softmax statistics. The two papers arrive at the same idea from the system and kernel sides.
- **MegaScale (Jiang et al., NSDI 2024).** Its abstract reports 55.2% MFU training a 175B model on 12,288 GPUs (1.34× Megatron-LM) through algorithm–system co-design (overlap, operator optimization, data pipeline, network tuning) and fault diagnosis. It builds on the Megatron 3D layout that this paper refines. Its contribution is more about overlap and reliability at more than 10K GPUs than about per-layer memory accounting.

## Takeaways for practice

- Use sbh(34 + 5as/h)/t as a quick sizing model per layer and per TP rank. Multiply by L (first stage, 1F1B) and by the interleave factor before choosing t, p and the microbatch size.
- Turn on SP whenever TP is on. It costs no extra communication bandwidth (and measured slightly faster in Table 4) and removes the 10sbh replicated term.
- Recompute what is cheap in FLOPs and expensive in bytes, not whole layers. With fused attention that region is already gone, so prefer no recomputation, or recompute only targeted ops such as GeLU or LN.
- The memory headroom from avoiding recomputation is worth about 30% of step time at 175B–1T. A scheduler or autotuner should treat the recompute policy as a first-class knob next to t, p and DP.
- Keep TP+SP inside an NVLink domain. SP's reduce-scatter and all-gather sit on the critical path just like TP's all-reduce did.
- Report MFU rather than HFU when comparing recompute policies. HFU rewards recomputed FLOPs, and even this paper's HFU numbers changed between versions.

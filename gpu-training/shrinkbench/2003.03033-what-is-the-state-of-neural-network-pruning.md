# What is the State of Neural Network Pruning?

## TL;DR

A meta-analysis of 81 neural network pruning papers that finds the field lacks standardized benchmarks and metrics, making progress hard to measure, and introduces ShrinkBench, an open-source framework for standardized pruning evaluation.

## Problem & Motivation

Neural network pruning research has produced many approaches over three decades, but results are reported under inconsistent experimental conditions, datasets, architectures, and metrics. This makes it nearly impossible to compare pruning techniques against one another or to judge how much real progress the field has made.

The authors argue this deficiency is substantial enough to undermine the literature itself, and that without controlled, standardized evaluation, reported gains may reflect setup differences rather than genuine methodological improvements.

## Key Ideas

- Aggregate results across 81 pruning papers to survey approaches and identify consistent findings.
- Re-prune hundreds of models under controlled conditions to test whether published comparisons hold up.
- Central finding: the community lacks standardized benchmarks and metrics, not just better algorithms.
- Identify specific flaws in current evaluation practices and propose concrete remedies.
- Introduce ShrinkBench, an open-source framework enabling standardized pruning evaluations.
- Demonstrate that comprehensive evaluation via ShrinkBench exposes pitfalls in naive method comparisons.

## System Design

- ShrinkBench is an open-source software framework for evaluating pruning methods under standardized conditions.
- It provides a common harness so different pruning techniques can be trained, pruned, and compared on equal footing.
- The paper pairs the framework with a meta-analysis pipeline: aggregating reported results from 81 papers and re-running pruning across hundreds of models in controlled settings.
- Evaluation output is designed to be comprehensive enough to reveal comparison pitfalls that narrower protocols miss.

## Evaluation

- Meta-analysis of 81 papers plus controlled pruning of hundreds of models.
- Compares various pruning techniques using ShrinkBench.
- Headline result from the abstract: the clearest finding is the lack of standardized benchmarks and metrics, severe enough that comparing techniques or measuring three decades of progress is difficult.
- Shows that ShrinkBench's comprehensive evaluation can prevent common pitfalls when comparing pruning methods.

## Takeaways

- Reported pruning gains are often not comparable across papers due to differing benchmarks, metrics, and setups.
- Standardization is a prerequisite for measuring real progress in pruning.
- Controlled re-evaluation of hundreds of models is feasible and reveals issues invisible in individual papers.
- Open-source tooling (ShrinkBench) is a practical remedy the community can adopt.
- Comprehensive evaluation protocols catch comparison pitfalls that narrow ones miss.

## Open Questions

- Whether the community will converge on the proposed standardized benchmarks and metrics.
- How well ShrinkBench's controlled settings generalize to the full diversity of modern architectures and deployment constraints.
- Which pruning approaches remain competitive once evaluated under standardized conditions.
- How to extend standardization to metrics beyond size/accuracy trade-offs, such as latency and energy.

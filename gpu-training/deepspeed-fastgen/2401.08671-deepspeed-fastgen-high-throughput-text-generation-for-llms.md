# DeepSpeed-FastGen: High-throughput Text Generation for LLMs via MII and DeepSpeed-Inference

> **Why it matters:** It turns "keep every forward pass at a fixed token count" into the scheduling rule for an LLM server. The rule rests on a simple throughput-vs-tokens performance model, and it became the chunked-prefill and mixed-batch design that most later engines use.

## Problem

Continuous batching (Orca) and blocked KV caches (vLLM's PagedAttention) had already raised serving throughput. The open question was how to combine prefill and decode work in one batch (§2.2). The paper names two failure modes:

- **Preempting decodes for prompts** (vLLM and TGI at the time). Whenever a new prompt arrives, running generations stall. This causes token-latency spikes and high tail latency.
- **Running whole prompts alongside decodes** (Orca). A long prompt makes that iteration expensive, so the decodes sharing it slow down.

Either way, forward-pass sizes swing between tiny decode-only batches and huge prompt batches, which hurts both throughput and latency consistency.

## Core design

### Performance model (§3.1, Figs. 1–2)

The paper makes three claims, all measured on its own kernels:

1. **Token count drives latency (§3.1.1, Fig. 1).** Forward-pass latency depends mainly on the total number of tokens in the pass, not on how many sequences they come from. A scheduler can therefore work from one signal: tokens per pass.
2. **Throughput saturates (§3.1.2, Fig. 2).** With few tokens, the pass is memory-bound by weight reads, so throughput rises roughly linearly with token count. Past a fairly sharp knee, the pass is compute-bound and throughput plateaus. The goal is to keep every pass just inside the plateau.
3. **Even splits are best (§3.1.3).** For "well-aligned" inputs the token-to-throughput curve is concave. Spreading P pending tokens over F forward passes therefore gives the most throughput when each pass gets P/F tokens. Concavity makes an uneven split (one big pass, one tiny pass) strictly worse. This is the formal argument for a **fixed token budget per pass**. The paper offers no proof beyond the concavity observation, and it does not examine misaligned shapes such as tile-quantization effects.

### Dynamic SplitFuse (§3.2, Fig. 3)

- **Split:** A long prompt is cut into chunks and processed over several forward passes. Only the final chunk's pass emits the first generated token. Earlier chunks just fill the KV cache.
- **Fuse:** Short prompts, and the decode tokens of running sequences, are packed together to fill the target token budget exactly. A short prompt may also be split so the budget is met precisely.
- **No preemption:** Decodes are never paused for prefill. A new prompt simply takes part of each pass's budget.
- **Claimed effects:** Better responsiveness, every pass in the high-throughput region, and lower latency variance. Fig. 3 contrasts vLLM, Orca and SplitFuse batch shapes.
- **Budget value:** The paper never states a numeric budget. §3.2 speaks only of a "target token budget".

### Blocked KV cache (§2.1)

FastGen uses a non-contiguous, block-based KV cache, as vLLM, TGI and TensorRT-LLM do. §2.1 motivates it by the fragmentation of monolithic per-sequence KV buffers, which caps concurrency. The paper gives no block size or allocator details and credits PagedAttention for the idea.

### MII + DeepSpeed-Inference serving layer (§5, Fig. 11)

- **DeepSpeed-Inference:** Supplies the optimized kernels (shipped as the precompiled DeepSpeed-Kernels wheel for compute capability 8.0+, §5.3), model-construction tools and the device-side infrastructure that runs SplitFuse batches.
- **DeepSpeed-MII:** Supplies the frontend APIs and host-side scheduling. It has two modes:
  - A **non-persistent** in-process `pipeline()` call (§5.2.1).
  - A **persistent** deployment (§5.2.2). `mii.serve()` starts a lightweight gRPC server with a built-in replica-level load balancer, and clients connect through `mii.client()`.
- **Models supported at release (§5.1):** LLaMA, LLaMA-2, Mistral and OPT. The follow-up blog adds Mixtral, Phi-2 and Falcon.

**Exact vLLM comparison settings (§4.1):**

- **Load:** 1 to 32 concurrent clients, 512 requests in total. Latency is measured per request at the endpoint. Throughput is end-to-end experiment time (§4.1.1).
- **Lengths:** Prompt and generation lengths are drawn from normal distributions with 30% variance. The means are 1200 or 2600 prompt tokens and 60 or 128 generated tokens, depending on the figure.
- **Effective-throughput SLA (§4.1.2):** Prompt processing must run at least 512 tokens/s (prompt latency ≤ prompt_tokens/512 s). The generation EMA must reach 2, 4 or 6 tokens/s. Only requests meeting both SLAs count.
- **Hardware:** Llama-2 70B with TP=4 on 4x A100-80GB; Llama-2 13B on 1x A100-80GB (Fig. 5); Llama-2 70B on 8x H100 (Fig. 9); Llama-2 7B on A6000 (Fig. 10). For H100 and A6000, §4.6 says only that trends match A100 and gives no numbers.
- **Not reported:** the vLLM version or configuration (block size, max batched tokens, scheduler flags), FastGen's token budget, warm-up, and which SLA tier produces the 2.3x headline. The blog lists releasing the benchmark code as future work.

## Limitations

- **Vendor benchmark against one baseline.** The only baseline is vLLM, at an unstated version from late 2023, and vLLM shipped chunked prefill soon after. The results compare a 2023 snapshot, not the two scheduling policies in general. There is no comparison with TensorRT-LLM, TGI or SARATHI, though all are cited.
- **The performance model is asserted, not derived.** Figs. 1–2 come from unnamed models and hardware. Concavity holds only "for well-aligned inputs". Kernel tile quantization can break it, and Sarathi-Serve later measured a 257-token chunk costing 32% more than a 256-token one.
- **No budget-selection method.** One fixed budget ignores the TBT-vs-throughput tradeoff. Sarathi-Serve sets the budget from a latency SLO and the profiled hardware instead.
- **Chunking overhead is unquantified.** Later chunks re-read earlier chunks' KV, and the paper never measures this cost.
- **Report quality.** In §§4.2–4.5 several in-text figure references are off. The ~2x average-latency claim has no figure.
- **Simple scaling.** Load balancing spreads requests evenly across replicas, with no KV-aware routing or prefill/decode disaggregation.

## Relation to other systems

- **Orca (OSDI 2022):** Introduced iteration-level scheduling and selective batching, beating FasterTransformer on GPT-3 175B at equal latency. FastGen keeps iteration-level scheduling but changes what goes into each iteration. Orca runs whole prompts with decodes, so pass size varies. FastGen caps every pass at a fixed token count.
- **vLLM / PagedAttention (SOSP 2023):** Contributed block-based KV memory with near-zero waste and 2–4x throughput over FasterTransformer and Orca. FastGen adopts the blocked KV idea. Its gain over vLLM comes from the scheduler: no prefill-first preemption of decodes.
- **SARATHI (arXiv 2308.16369):** Proposed chunked prefills plus decode-maximal batching, in which one prefill chunk is filled out with piggybacked decodes. It reported up to 10x decode throughput on LLaMA-13B/A6000 and smaller pipeline bubbles. FastGen cites it as "a similar approach" (§3.2). SplitFuse adds fusing several short prompts into one budget and frames everything as a token budget, with no single-prefill-chunk rule.
- **Sarathi-Serve (OSDI 2024):** Generalizes SARATHI with stall-free scheduling and near-equal chunks, and picks the token budget as the largest that meets the TBT SLO, taking tile sizes into account. It reports up to 2.6x (Mistral-7B, 1 A100) and 3.7x (Yi-34B, 2 A100s) serving capacity over vLLM, with Orca and vLLM as baselines. It is in effect a peer-reviewed, SLO-aware SplitFuse, though it does not cite FastGen.
- **TensorRT-LLM:** Named only as another system with a non-contiguous KV cache and continuous batching (§§2.1–2.2). No measurements are given.

## Takeaways for practice

- Schedule by **tokens per forward pass**, not requests or sequences. One knob, the token budget, controls both throughput and per-token latency.
- Profile your model and GPU for the throughput-vs-tokens knee, and keep the budget just past it: below it, passes waste bandwidth; far above it, they add latency without throughput.
- Never stall running decodes to admit prompts. Chunk prefills and mix them in. Tail TBT is what users and SLAs notice.
- Pick the budget from your latency SLO and kernel tile sizes (the Sarathi-Serve method), not one global constant. Re-tune it when model, TP degree or GPU changes.
- Define and report **effective throughput under SLA**, not raw rps. It exposes preemption-driven SLA misses that averages hide.
- Treat cross-vendor serving benchmarks as configuration-sensitive. Pin baseline versions and flags, and rerun them yourself before acting on headline ratios.

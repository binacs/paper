# A System for Massively Parallel Hyperparameter Tuning

## TL;DR

Introduces ASHA, a simple and robust hyperparameter optimization algorithm that exploits parallelism and aggressive early-stopping, and describes its integration into Determined AI's production ML system.

## Problem & Motivation

Modern ML models have large hyperparameter spaces and long training times, making hyperparameter tuning computationally expensive. The rise of parallel computing and the need to productionize ML workloads demand efficient hyperparameter optimization in distributed settings, but existing methods often struggle with scalability and robustness.

## Key Ideas

- ASHA (Asynchronous Successive Halving Algorithm) extends Successive Halving to asynchronous, parallel settings.
- Aggressive early-stopping terminates poorly performing trials early to save resources.
- Linear scaling with number of workers enables massive parallelism (demonstrated with 500 workers).
- Simple design with minimal hyperparameters makes it robust and easy to use.

## System Design

ASHA is integrated into Determined AI's end-to-end ML system. The system manages a cluster of workers, each running training trials. A central scheduler assigns hyperparameter configurations and monitors trial progress. ASHA uses a promotion-based scheme: trials are promoted to higher rungs (longer training budgets) based on performance relative to peers. The system handles fault tolerance, resource allocation, and data management.

## Evaluation

Extensive empirical results on multiple benchmarks show ASHA outperforms state-of-the-art methods like Bayesian optimization and Hyperband. It scales linearly with workers, achieving near-ideal speedups up to 500 workers. Headline numbers include up to 10x speedup over random search on some tasks.

## Takeaways

- Aggressive early-stopping is key to efficiency in large-scale hyperparameter tuning.
- Asynchronous parallelism avoids synchronization overhead and scales well.
- Simplicity (few hyperparameters) makes ASHA robust and practical for production.
- Integration into a full ML system requires careful design for fault tolerance and resource management.

## Related Work

ASHA builds on Successive Halving and Hyperband, but adapts them for asynchronous, distributed settings. It outperforms Bayesian optimization methods like GP-EI and SMAC in the evaluated scenarios.

## Open Questions

- How does ASHA perform on extremely high-dimensional hyperparameter spaces?
- Can the early-stopping criterion be improved to avoid premature termination of promising configurations?
- How does ASHA compare to more recent methods like BOHB or population-based training?
- What are the trade-offs between aggressiveness of early-stopping and final model quality?

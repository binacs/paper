# FlashAttention-2: Faster Attention with Better Parallelism and Work Partitioning

> **Why it matters:** It keeps FlashAttention's exact, IO-aware tiling but re-partitions the work across thread blocks and warps, roughly doubling attention throughput on A100 and becoming the default attention kernel in most training and serving stacks.

## Problem

Attention cost grows quadratically with sequence length. FlashAttention (FA-1) cut memory to linear and gave 2–4x speedups by tiling, but on A100 it reached only 30–50% of peak FLOPs/s in the forward pass and 25–35% in the backward pass (Sec. 1), against 80–90% for a tuned GEMM. The paper attributes the gap to how work is split, not to IO: (a) too many non-matmul FLOPs, which run far slower than Tensor Core matmuls; (b) one thread block per (batch, head), so long-sequence, small-batch runs leave SMs idle; (c) a warp split inside each block that forces round-trips through shared memory.

## Core design

### Recap: IO-aware tiling (FA-1)

Standard attention materializes S = QKᵀ and P = softmax(S) (N×N) in HBM. FA-1 instead loads blocks of Q, K, V into on-chip SRAM, computes a block of S, and uses an online softmax: it keeps a running row max m and row sum ℓ, rescales the partial output as new K/V blocks arrive, and never writes S or P to HBM. The backward pass recomputes S and P block by block from Q, K, V and the saved softmax statistics, trading extra FLOPs for HBM traffic. The result is exact, with O(N²d) FLOPs and O(N) extra memory (Sec. 2.3, 3.1).

### Change 1: fewer non-matmul FLOPs (Sec. 3.1)

On A100, FP16/BF16 matmul peaks at 312 TFLOPs/s against 19.5 TFLOPs/s for non-matmul FP32, so each non-matmul FLOP costs about 16x a matmul FLOP. FA-2 changes the online softmax so that:
- The output accumulator stays unscaled during the inner loop and is divided by the final ℓ once at the end, instead of rescaling by diag(ℓ)⁻¹ on every block.
- Only the logsumexp L = m + log ℓ is saved for backward, instead of both m and ℓ.
- With a causal mask, blocks entirely above the diagonal are skipped (about 1.7–1.8x speedup over not skipping, Sec. 3.1.1), and the mask is applied only to blocks that straddle the diagonal.
- MQA/GQA are handled by index arithmetic over K/V heads rather than copying K/V; backward sums dK and dV over the heads that share them (Sec. 3.1.2).

### Change 2: parallelism over sequence length (Sec. 3.2)

FA-1 launches batch × heads thread blocks. That schedules well when the count is large (the paper says ≥ 80, on an A100 with 108 SMs), but long sequences usually mean small batches and few heads per GPU. FA-2 also splits over the sequence dimension:
- **Forward:** the outer loop is over row blocks of Q; each thread block owns one row block and loops over all K/V column blocks. Row blocks are independent, so there is no communication between blocks. The paper credits Phil Tillet's Triton implementation for this loop order.
- **Backward:** each thread block owns a column block of K/V and accumulates dK and dV locally; the shared dQ is updated across blocks with atomic adds.

### Change 3: warp work partitioning (Sec. 3.3)

Inside a block (usually 4 or 8 warps), FA-1 used "split-K": K and V are split across warps, Q is shared, and the warps' partial QKᵀ·V results must be written to shared memory, synchronized and summed. FA-2 splits Q across warps and lets all warps share K/V. Each warp then computes a full slice of rows of the output, with no warp-to-warp communication in the forward pass. Backward also avoids split-K, though dependencies among Q, K, V, O, dO, dQ, dK, dV still require some synchronization. Block sizes are {64, 128} × {64, 128}, hand-tuned per head dimension: bigger tiles cut shared-memory traffic but raise register and SRAM pressure, and past a limit the kernel spills registers or cannot launch.

### Forward vs backward

The forward pass is the cleaner case: independent row blocks and communication-free warps. The backward pass does 5 matmuls against 2 in the forward pass, counting recomputation (Sec. 4.1). It must also reduce dQ across column blocks with atomics, so its FLOP utilization stays below the forward pass's throughout Figs. 5–6. Against Triton FA, the gain is about 1.3–1.5x in forward and about 2x in backward (Sec. 4.1).

## Limitations

- **Hardware-specific tuning.** Block sizes are tuned by hand per head dimension (Sec. 3.3), and the kernel targets Ampere. On H100 it uses no TMA, 4th-gen Tensor Core or FP8 features; the paper lists these, AMD support and compiler-based portability as future work (Sec. 5). FA-3 later reported that FA-2 reaches only about 35% utilization on H100.
- **Backward still lags forward.** dQ atomics and the residual synchronization keep backward below forward in every panel of Figs. 5–6.
- **Short-sequence end-to-end gains are small.** At 2k context the gain over FA-1 is 196 vs 189 and 205 vs 189 TFLOPs/s (Table 1). Most of the headline gain appears at 8k, where attention is a larger share of FLOPs.
- **FLOP accounting.** The end-to-end MFU uses the Megatron-LM formula and deliberately does not halve the attention term for causal masking (Sec. 4.2). The 72% MFU therefore counts attention FLOPs the kernel skips. *(Own analysis:* the paper states this convention openly, but it flatters causal-LM utilization relative to an "actually executed FLOPs" count.)*
- **Narrow evaluation.** *(Own analysis:)* End-to-end results cover only two GPT-3-style sizes on a single 8×A100 node, with no multi-node runs, no inference/decode workloads and no variable-length or padded batches. The atomic dQ reduction also makes backward results run-to-run non-deterministic in floating-point summation order, which the paper does not discuss.
- **Dropout and masking are omitted** from the presented algorithms for clarity (footnote 2), so their overheads are not analyzed.

## Relation to other systems

- **FlashAttention (NeurIPS 2022).** Introduced IO-aware exact attention with tiling and recomputation, provably fewer HBM accesses, and reported 3x on GPT-2 (1K) and 15% over the MLPerf 1.1 BERT-large record. FA-2 keeps the same IO complexity and math. It changes only the loop order (Q-outer), the grid (sequence-parallel blocks), the warp split (split-Q instead of split-K) and the softmax bookkeeping.
- **FlashAttention-3 (NeurIPS 2024).** Targets Hopper explicitly. It adds warp specialization to overlap TMA data movement with WGMMA compute, interleaves block-wise matmul and softmax, and uses FP8 with block quantization and incoherent processing. It reports 1.5–2.0x over FA-2 on H100, up to 740 TFLOPs/s in FP16 (about 75% utilization) and close to 1.2 PFLOPs/s in FP8. FA-2's design is synchronous within a warp; FA-3's main change is asynchrony.
- **PagedAttention / vLLM (SOSP 2023).** Solves a different bottleneck: KV-cache fragmentation and duplication in serving. It pages the KV cache like OS virtual memory and reports 2–4x throughput over FasterTransformer and Orca at equal latency. FA-2 assumes contiguous Q/K/V tiles and targets training-shape prefill compute. PagedAttention changes memory layout and indirection for decode, so the two are complementary rather than competing.
- **Megatron activation recomputation (MLSys 2023).** Reduces activation memory at the model-parallel level with sequence parallelism and selective recomputation: 5x less activation memory and 54.2% MFU on a 530B model across 2,240 A100s. Its main target is avoiding recomputation of the attention softmax/dropout activations. FA-style kernels never materialize those N×N activations at all, which removes much of the reason for selective recomputation of attention. FA-2 borrows Megatron's FLOP formula for its MFU numbers.
- **xFormers memory-efficient attention.** The docs describe `xformers.ops.memory_efficient_attention` as exact attention that dispatches among its own CUDA kernels and other libraries, Flash-Attention among them. Its algorithmic root, Rabe & Staats, showed attention needs only O(√n) memory in practice, with a 59x/32x memory reduction at 16k for inference/differentiation. FA-2 benchmarks against xFormers' "cutlass" kernel (Figs. 4–6) and outperforms it. The two differ in scope: xFormers is a dispatcher and library of building blocks, while FA-2 is a single tuned kernel design.

## Takeaways for practice

- Once IO is fixed, the next bottleneck is **occupancy and intra-SM communication**. For long-context, small-batch jobs, check that the attention grid covers the SMs. Sequence-dimension parallelism is the fix, and it matters most for exactly the jobs that context-parallel schedulers create.
- **Non-matmul work is expensive** (about 16x per FLOP on A100). Moving any elementwise rescaling out of inner loops, or fusing it into matmul epilogues, pays off directly.
- **Expect end-to-end gains to scale with context length.** Budget capacity on the basis of 8k+ runs, not 2k: at 2k the gain over FA-1 is a few percent.
- **Kernel-level gains do not carry over to new hardware for free.** The same FA-2 code reaches only about a third of H100 peak, so cluster cost models should track kernel version × GPU generation, not GPU generation alone.
- **When comparing MFU across stacks, check the FLOP convention** (causal halving, recomputation counted or not) before you compare.
- **Atomics in backward imply non-bitwise-reproducible gradients.** Plan for this in determinism tests and checkpoint-replay tooling *(own analysis)*.

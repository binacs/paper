# Taming Throughput-Latency Tradeoff in LLM Inference with Sarathi-Serve

## TL;DR

Sarathi-Serve is an LLM inference scheduler that uses chunked-prefills and stall-free scheduling to simultaneously achieve high throughput and low latency, significantly outperforming vLLM across various models and hardware.

## Problem & Motivation

LLM serving requests have two phases: prefill (high latency, high GPU utilization) and decode (low latency, low GPU utilization). Batching multiple requests interleaves prefill and decode iterations, creating a tradeoff between throughput and latency. Existing systems struggle to achieve both high throughput and low latency under tail latency constraints.

## Key Ideas

- Chunked-prefills: Split a prefill request into near equal-sized chunks to reduce prefill latency and enable better batching.
- Stall-free scheduling: Add new requests to a batch without pausing ongoing decode iterations, eliminating pipeline stalls.
- Uniform batches: Create batches with similar compute characteristics to minimize pipeline bubbles and improve utilization.

## System Design

Sarathi-Serve is a scheduler for LLM inference. It processes requests by splitting prefill into chunks and interleaving them with decode iterations in a stall-free manner. The scheduler forms uniform batches that balance compute across iterations, reducing pipeline bubbles. The system is designed for single-GPU and pipeline-parallel deployments.

## Evaluation

Evaluated on Mistral-7B (single A100), Yi-34B (two A100s), and Falcon-180B (pipeline parallelism). Baselines include vLLM. Headline results: 2.6x higher serving capacity for Mistral-7B, up to 3.7x for Yi-34B, and up to 5.6x for Falcon-180B under tail latency constraints.

## Takeaways

- Chunked-prefills effectively reduce prefill latency and enable better batching.
- Stall-free scheduling is key to achieving both high throughput and low latency.
- Uniform batches minimize pipeline bubbles, improving overall efficiency.
- Sarathi-Serve significantly outperforms vLLM across diverse models and hardware configurations.

## Related Work

Sarathi-Serve is positioned against vLLM, a state-of-the-art LLM serving system. It addresses the throughput-latency tradeoff that vLLM and other systems struggle with by introducing chunked-prefills and stall-free scheduling.

## Open Questions

How does Sarathi-Serve perform with more complex scheduling policies (e.g., fairness, request prioritization)? Can chunked-prefills be extended to other transformer architectures? What are the memory overheads of chunked-prefills?

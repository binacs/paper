# The Llama 3 Herd of Models (training infrastructure, §3.3)

> **Why it matters:** One of the few public, quantitative accounts of running a single dense-LLM pre-training job on 16K H100s over a production RoCE fabric, including 4D-parallel layouts with measured MFU and a root-cause breakdown of every unexpected job interruption over 54 days.

This note covers only the infrastructure part of the paper (§3.3 "Infrastructure, Scaling, and Efficiency"), not the model, data or post-training.

## Problem

Llama 3 405B is a dense Transformer pre-trained on up to 16K GPUs. Llama 1 and 2 ran on Meta's AI Research SuperCluster; Llama 3 moved to Meta's production clusters (§3.3.1). The move raised four problems:

- **One synchronous job:** a single slow or failed component stalls thousands of GPUs.
- **Network:** the 405B run used RoCE with an oversubscribed upper tier, and collective traffic is a few large flows that defeat ECMP hashing.
- **Balance:** memory and compute must balance across four parallel dimensions, for both 8K- and 128K-token phases.
- **Reliability:** hardware faults are routine at this scale, yet most wall-clock time must stay productive.

## Core design

### Compute, scheduling, storage (§3.3.1)
- **Hardware:** H100s on Grand Teton servers, eight GPUs and two CPUs per server, NVLink inside the server.
- **Scheduler:** MAST, Meta's global-scale training scheduler.
- **Storage:** Tectonic, Meta's distributed file system. Checkpoints write each GPU's model state, so writes are very bursty. The goals are less GPU pause per checkpoint and more frequent checkpoints, so less work is lost on recovery.

### Network (§3.3.1)
- **Fabrics:** the 405B model trained on RoCE (Arista 7800 and Minipack2 OCP switches); smaller models on NVIDIA Quantum2 InfiniBand.
- **Topology:** a three-layer Clos. 192 racks of 16 GPUs form a full-bisection pod; eight pods join through a 1:7 oversubscribed aggregation layer. MAST and the parallelism layout are network-aware, so heavy traffic stays inside a pod.
- **Load balancing:** the collective library opens 16 flows per GPU pair, and Enhanced-ECMP (E-ECMP) spreads them by hashing extra RoCE header fields.
- **Congestion control:** deep-buffer spines absorb transient congestion from collectives and slow servers. With E-ECMP, the 24K-GPU cluster ran without DCQCN.

### 4D parallelism (§3.3.2, Fig. 5)
- **Dimensions:** tensor (TP), context (CP), pipeline (PP) and data parallelism (FSDP). FSDP shards optimizer state and gradients but does not reshard weights after forward, saving an all-gather in backward.
- **Order:** [TP, CP, PP, DP] from inner to outer. TP stays on NVLink; DP, which can hide latency, is outermost. Configurations are picked with a memory-consumption estimator and a performance-projection tool.
- **Numerics:** FP32 gradient accumulation across micro-batches and FP32 reduce-scatter.

### Pipeline-parallel changes (§3.3.2, Fig. 6)
Existing schedules had batch-size constraints and memory and compute imbalance: the first stage holds the embedding and warm-up micro-batches, the last the output layer and loss. Fixes:
- **Tunable N** (contiguous micro-batches per round). Depth-first forces N = PP size and breadth-first forces N = total micro-batches; a free N (N=5 in the example) decouples batch size from pipeline depth.
- **Layer balancing:** one fewer Transformer layer on the first and last stages.
- **Interleaved schedule** with asynchronous point-to-point communication.
- **Memory:** TORCH_NCCL_AVOID_RECORD_STREAMS and proactive freeing of dead tensors allowed 8K-token pre-training without activation checkpointing.

### Context parallelism (§3.3.2)
- The sequence is cut into 2×CP chunks, two per rank, to balance the causal mask.
- **All-gather instead of ring.** K/V are all-gathered, then attention runs locally. This handles masks such as the document mask easily; GQA keeps K/V small, so at long context the all-gather is negligible next to attention compute.

### NCCLX (§3.3.3)
Meta's NCCL fork. Stock NCCL's chunking and staged copies create many small control messages and extra copies, which hurt when outer-dimension latency reaches tens of microseconds. NCCLX tunes chunking for that latency and gives control messages higher network priority to avoid head-of-line blocking.

### Reliability tooling (§3.3.4)
- **Automation:** most failures are handled automatically; only three needed manual intervention in 54 days.
- **PyTorch NCCL flight recorder:** a buffer of collective metadata for diagnosing hangs; heavier tracing can be enabled in production without a restart.
- **Mixed NVLink/RoCE failures:** communication-library state is monitored and stalled operations time out.
- **Stragglers:** suspicious communicator groups are ranked so the top suspects are checked first; one straggler can slow thousands of GPUs.
- **Environment:** daytime heat affects GPU DVFS, causing 1–2% diurnal throughput variation. Synchronized idling (checkpoints, collectives, job start/stop) swings power by tens of megawatts.

## Limitations

- **Thin on mechanism:** each component gets about a paragraph. There are no ablations (E-ECMP vs ECMP, NCCLX vs NCCL), no checkpoint or recovery times, and no breakdown of the ">90%" effective training time.
- **Table 5 arithmetic:** "Faulty GPU 148 / 30.1%" is inconsistent; 148/419 ≈ 35.3%, while other rows match count/419 and the counts sum to 419. The 58.7% GPU share is the sum of the printed percentages; raw counts give 268/419 ≈ 64%. (My arithmetic, not the paper's.)
- **MFU drop unexplained:** config C (128K context) falls to 38%, with no split between CP communication, bubbles and other overheads.
- **Dense only:** MoE all-to-all and expert imbalance are out of scope.
- **No DCQCN:** this depends on deep buffers and a known single-job traffic pattern; multi-tenant clusters are not shown.
- **Not reproducible:** MAST, Tectonic and NCCLX are not released with the paper.

## Relation to other systems

- **Megatron-LM:** origin of TP+PP and the interleaved schedule. Llama 3 adds a tunable N and trimmed end stages, and does CP by all-gather rather than ring exchange.
- **PyTorch FSDP / ZeRO:** the DP axis keeps weights unsharded after forward (ZeRO-2-like for parameters), trading memory for one fewer all-gather.
- **Ring Attention:** overlaps K/V passing with compute; Llama 3 argues GQA makes a plain all-gather cheap and simpler for arbitrary masks.
- **MegaScale (ByteDance, NSDI'24):** a comparable 10K+ GPU production report that stresses diagnosis and fast restart. Llama 3 gives a finer failure taxonomy but less on recovery.
- **Meta RoCE paper (SIGCOMM'24, "RDMA over Ethernet for Distributed AI Training at Meta Scale"):** the fuller account of the same fabric, E-ECMP and running without DCQCN.
- **Alibaba HPN (SIGCOMM'24):** an Ethernet fabric with dual-plane, rail-optimized tiers, not an oversubscribed Clos with network-aware placement.

## Takeaways for practice

- **Map parallelism to the network:** order dimensions [TP, CP, PP, DP] from inner to outer and have the scheduler keep heavy traffic inside a full-bisection pod; then a 1:7 upper tier is acceptable.
- **Add flow entropy:** 16 flows per GPU pair plus extra hash fields is a cheap fix for RoCE ECMP collisions.
- **Expect daily failures, mostly GPU and HBM:** invest in automated detection, eviction and restart, and in frequent low-pause checkpoints.
- **Hangs and stragglers are the hard cases:** collective flight recorders, tracing without restart and suspect ranking make them debuggable.
- **Plan for power steps:** swings of tens of megawatts and thermal effects belong in capacity planning.
- **MFU baseline:** 38–43% BF16 MFU at 8K–16K H100s (Table 4).

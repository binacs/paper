# DistServe: Disaggregating Prefill and Decoding for Goodput-optimized Large Language Model Serving

> **Why it matters:** It reframes LLM serving as maximizing per-GPU goodput under separate TTFT and TPOT SLOs, and shows that putting prefill and decode on different GPUs, each with its own parallelism plan and a bandwidth-aware placement, beats colocated continuous batching by large factors.

## Problem

Prefill processes the whole prompt in one compute-bound step and yields the first token. Decode then emits one token per step and is memory-bandwidth-bound. Users see two latencies: TTFT (time to first token) and TPOT (time per output token). vLLM and Orca colocate both phases on the same GPUs under continuous batching, which causes two problems (§2.3):

- **Interference.** Adding even one prefill job to a decode batch slows both phases, more so for longer prompts (Fig. 2). Prioritizing either phase in the scheduler only moves the cost to the other SLO.
- **Coupled resources and parallelism.** Prefill wants intra-op (tensor) parallelism to cut TTFT. The best decode plan depends on batch size and the TPOT target. Colocation forces one plan and one GPU pool to satisfy the stricter SLO, so operators over-provision.

Chunked prefill with piggybacking (SARATHI, DeepSpeed-MII) mitigates the problem but does not remove it (§2.3). Small chunks slow prefill, and near-saturating chunks leave little room for decode tokens. N chunks also mean O(N²) KV reloads instead of O(N).

The paper's metric is **goodput**: the maximum request rate per provisioned GPU at which a target fraction (e.g. 90%) of requests meets both the TTFT and TPOT SLOs (§1).

## Core design

### Disaggregation

Prefill and decode instances run on separate GPUs. The prefill instance computes the KV cache and first token, and a decode instance continues from the moved KV cache. Each phase has its own replica count and (intra-op, inter-op) degrees.

### Per-phase tradeoff analysis (§3)

- **Prefill.** Batch only below a profiled length L_m where prefill becomes compute-bound; for a 13B model, one 512-token sequence already saturates an A100 (§2.1, §3.1). An M/D/1 queue model gives Avg_TTFT = D + RD²/(2(1−RD)) (Eq. 1), with variants for 2-way inter-op (Eq. 2) and intra-op with speedup 1 < K < 2 (Eq. 3). Intra-op wins at low rates and tight TTFT SLOs, inter-op at higher rates, and a lower K shrinks intra-op's benefit (Fig. 4, 66B on two A100s).
- **Decode.** Bandwidth-bound, so large batches matter, and disaggregation lets decode build them without prefill stalls. Intra-op cuts latency with diminishing returns; inter-op scales throughput almost linearly (Fig. 5). Use intra-op only as far as the TPOT SLO requires, then inter-op or replication.

### Placement search (§4)

Given the model, a workload trace, the SLOs and the attainment target, DistServe picks a "placement" (per-phase parallelism, instance counts, node mapping) that maximizes per-GPU goodput.

- **Simulator.** SLO attainment has no closed form, so DistServe fits a distribution to a historical trace, resamples it, and runs an event simulator over an analytical latency model (App. A), e.g. T_prefill = C1·(4th² + 2thm) + C2·(3ht²/b) + C3, with constants fitted by profiling. Binary search finds the maximum rate that meets the attainment target.
- **Algorithm 1, high node-affinity** (InfiniBand-class links). Prefill and decode are optimized independently: enumerate intra-op 1..M and inter-op up to N·M/intra, keep configurations that fit in memory, simulate each, and replicate the best per-GPU configuration ⌈R/goodput⌉ times. O(NM²).
- **Algorithm 2, low node-affinity** (weak cross-node bandwidth). KV only moves between corresponding layers, so each instance is cut into per-pipeline-stage segments and the prefill and decode segments of the same stage share a node; all KV traffic rides NVLink. The search enumerates inter-op 1..N, then pairs of intra-node prefill and decode configurations that fit in M GPUs, simulates each pair, and replicates the best.

### Online scheduling (§4.3)

- A central controller routes each request to the shortest-queue prefill instance, then to the least-loaded decode instance; both are FCFS.
- **Pipeline bubbles.** Prefill batches are sized to total length ≈ L_m (short prompts grouped, long ones alone); for decode, L_m is the maximum batch size.
- **Burstiness.** Decode instances *pull* KV caches when they have capacity, so prefill memory is the queueing buffer and bursts cannot overflow decode memory.
- **Replanning.** A profiler tracks lengths and arrival rate and reruns placement when the workload shifts.
- No preemption or fault tolerance (§4.3, §7).

### Implementation (§5)

6.5K lines of Python (placement, OpenAI-compatible frontend, orchestration) and 8.1K lines of C++/CUDA (Ray-actor engine with continuous batching, FlashAttention, PagedAttention; OPT and LLaMA). KV moves via NCCL across nodes and asynchronous cudaMemcpy within a node.

## Limitations

- **SLO-centric.** For offline, throughput-oriented jobs the paper concedes chunked prefill may be preferable (§7).
- **Needs scale.** With few GPUs the design space collapses and a colocated system may win (§7).
- **KV transfer is cheap only by construction.** The negligible overhead relies on Alg. 2 pinning matching stage segments to one node (NVLink); Alg. 1 assumes InfiniBand-class links (§3.3, §4.2). Mooncake later makes KV movement and caching the central problem.
- **FCFS, no preemption.** Long prefills can convoy short ones; a failed decode instance shared by several prefill instances spreads disruption (§4.3, §7).
- **Coarse adaptation.** Replanning reruns the search on drift; no fine-grained elastic role switching.
- **Evaluation scope.** OPT/FP16 on A100s, one 32-GPU cluster, two baselines; no GQA, MoE or prefix caching. §6.5 swaps the Alg. 1/Alg. 2 labels used in §4.

## Relation to other systems

- **vLLM (SOSP'23).** PagedAttention with colocated continuous batching, intra-op only. DistServe reuses PagedAttention per instance; vLLM++ with tuned parallelism gains nothing, since intra-op=4 was already best (§6.4), so colocation is the problem.
- **Sarathi-Serve (OSDI'24).** Stays colocated, using near-equal prefill chunks and stall-free scheduling; its abstract reports up to 2.6×/3.7×/5.6× capacity over vLLM (Mistral-7B, Yi-34B, Falcon-180B). DistServe argues chunking cannot remove interference and adds O(N²) KV reads (§2.3), but concedes it suits throughput-oriented serving (§7).
- **Splitwise (ISCA'24).** Concurrent phase split across machines, with the token phase on cheaper hardware; its abstract reports 1.4× throughput at 20% lower cost, or 2.35× at equal cost and power. DistServe says it differs by targeting SLO goodput and bandwidth-aware placement (§8).
- **TetriInfer (arXiv 2401.11181).** Concurrent; disaggregation plus fixed-size prefill chunks and prediction-based two-level scheduling against decode hotspots; its abstract reports 97% lower average TTFT and 38% fewer resources. DistServe instead does an offline, simulator-driven parallelism and placement search.
- **Mooncake (arXiv 2407.00079).** Kimi's production P/D disaggregation around a KV cache pooled from CPU, DRAM and SSD, with a KV-centric scheduler and early rejection; its abstract reports up to 525% throughput in simulation and 75% more real requests. It generalizes "move the KV once" to "cache and reuse KV cluster-wide".

## Takeaways for practice

- Measure serving by **goodput under both TTFT and TPOT SLOs**, not tokens/s; chat, code and summarization want very different SLO pairs (Table 1).
- Run prefill and decode as **separately scaled services** with their own replicas and TP/PP plans (OPT-66B: prefill TP4, decode TP2×PP2).
- **Feed topology to the scheduler.** With weak cross-node links, colocate matching stage segments so KV rides NVLink; transfer then stays under 0.1% of latency at 25 Gbps cross-node.
- Use **pull-based KV handoff** so prefill memory absorbs bursts.
- An **analytical latency model plus trace-driven simulator** (<2% error) makes placement search take minutes; the pattern carries over to other GPU fleet configuration searches.
- Keep a colocated or chunked-prefill path for small or offline deployments.

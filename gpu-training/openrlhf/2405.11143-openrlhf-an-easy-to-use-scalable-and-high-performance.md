# OpenRLHF: An Easy-to-use, Scalable and High-performance RLHF Framework

> **Why it matters:** OpenRLHF is the reference open-source answer to "how do I schedule four LLM roles plus a vLLM rollout engine over one GPU cluster", and its Ray-role + ZeRO + vLLM layering became the template that later RLHF/RLVR systems either copied or explicitly argued against.

Read against arXiv **v6** (9 Oct 2025, the latest) and the peer-reviewed version. It **did** appear at a peer-reviewed venue: *EMNLP 2025: System Demonstrations*, pp. 656–666, DOI `10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-demos.48` (ACL Anthology `2025.emnlp-demos.48`), under the variant title "OpenRLHF: **A Ray-based** Easy-to-use, …". The arXiv page carries no comments/journal-ref linking the two; content matches (same Table 1, same §5).

## Problem

RLHF/RLVR is not one model but a dataflow over four or five: the actor being trained, a critic, a frozen reference policy for the KL term, a reward model or verifier, and a generation engine. Earlier frameworks (DeepSpeed-Chat, TRL, ColossalChat) co-located all of them in one process and generated with the *training* stack. §1 argues this is slow — "the inference phase often accounts for over 90% of the total RLHF" runtime — and unscalable, and that alternatives were either too monolithic to extend or too large to learn. OpenRLHF's answer: Ray owns placement and dataflow, vLLM owns generation, DeepSpeed ZeRO owns training, and the glue stays small.

## Core design

**Roles, not a monolith (§3.1, Fig. 1).** OpenRLHF "assigns a batch of GPUs to distinct roles" and uses Ray to schedule data and control between them. The published version collapses this to two roles: a **rollout engine** (vLLM, generation) and a **ZeRO engine** (DeepSpeed; actor and critic training plus the forward passes for actor and reference log-probs). Reward model, reference policy and critic appear only as computations that can be parallelized and batched; the paper gives **no** role-to-GPU map. The repo README is the concrete source: Ray is the "distributed scheduler and controller" and "separates the Actor, Reward, Reference, and Critic models across different GPUs", targeting 70B+. Treat the four-separate-actors picture as repo-documented, not paper-documented.

**Generation (§3.2).** vLLM is the rollout engine, for PagedAttention plus continuous batching, CUDA Graph, FlashAttention/FlashInfer, speculative decoding and chunked prefill; its PagedAttention figures (<4% KV-cache waste, up to 55% memory saved for parallel sampling/beam search) are inherited vLLM claims, not OpenRLHF measurements. The design point is that vLLM exposes "a streamlined interface for generating RLHF samples and supporting frequent model weight updates" — the rollout engine is a *mutable* service, not a static server.

**Training (§3.1, §3.2).** HF models under DeepSpeed ZeRO, with 3D parallelism assembled from heterogeneous pieces rather than a Megatron-style mesh: ZeRO/data parallelism for sharding, DeepSpeed **AutoTP** for tensor parallelism (no hand-written injection policy), ring attention for sequence parallelism on long contexts. Experiments use ZeRO-3 (§4.1); the repo exposes `--ds.zero_stage` and `--ds.ring_attn_size`.

**Weight sync between trainer and generator.** The weakest part of the text. §3.1 says only that model slices move into vLLM "through AutoTP and AutoPP", which "dynamically partition the models into sub-modules"; AutoPP is never defined, and **NCCL, CUDA IPC and broadcast appear nowhere in the paper**. The repo fills the gap: NCCL/CUDA IPC as inter-GPU transports, `--vllm.sync_backend nccl` recommended for vLLM weight syncing, and in async mode `--train.partial_rollout_enable` "uses vLLM pause/resume for weight sync instead of locking", so an in-flight sequence may carry tokens from two weight versions. In colocated mode the repo states every rollout uses the latest weights.

**Colocation vs separate placement.** The paper names "hybrid engine and asynchronous training" as modes Ray can switch between (§3.1) and notes rollout and ZeRO engines "can operate on different GPU clusters", pairing inference- with training-optimized hardware (appendix) — but offers no colocation-vs-disaggregation analysis and no placement ablation. The policy lives in the repo: `--train.colocate_all` (hybrid engine, all models and vLLM engines sharing GPUs, "best GPU utilization, strictly on-policy"), finer-grained `--train.colocate_actor_ref` and `--train.colocate_critic_reward`, and `--vllm.enable_sleep` / `--ds.enable_sleep` to park one engine's memory while the other runs. Async mode (`--train.async_enable`, `--train.async_queue_size` for off-policyness, `--algo.advantage.is_correction_enable` for IS correction) relaxes lockstep at a convergence cost the repo flags.

**Loop (appendix).** Per step: vLLM rollout → reward scoring with the KL penalty folded into the reward against the frozen reference → GAE → clipped surrogate + value loss (+ entropy) on the ZeRO engine, with KL-threshold early stopping. Recipes: PPO, GRPO, DAPO, plus SFT/DPO/RM/PRM training (§4.2); the repo adds REINFORCE++(-baseline), RLOO, Dr. GRPO and multi-turn agent rollouts.

## Limitations

- Stated (§5): may not reach the peak performance of specialized industrial frameworks; community-driven without dedicated funding, so features can lag; language-model-only, no multimodal support (the repo now claims VLM support, so this has moved); dependence on Ray/vLLM/DeepSpeed exposes it to upstream churn.
- **My own analysis, not a paper claim:** Table 1's averages do not reproduce. Recomputing the geometric mean of verl/OpenRLHF per-step times gives roughly 1.15× / 1.18× / 1.39× for 1.5B/7B/14B against the reported 1.22× / 1.42× / 1.68×. The two single-point ratios quoted in §4.1 (both 1.56×) do check out, so the gap is in aggregation, not the raw timings.
- **My own analysis:** the evaluation is single-node (8×H200) and ≤14B — exactly the regime where placement matters least. The distinguishing claim, Ray-scheduled separate placement of actor/critic/reward/reference at 70B+, is never measured, and there is no multi-node run and no ablation of colocated vs disaggregated, sync vs async, or partial rollout.
- **My own analysis:** the mechanisms a systems reader wants are under-specified — weight-transfer protocol and cost, resharding between ZeRO-3 shards and vLLM's TP layout, bubble accounting, staleness bounds. Baseline versions for TRL and DSChat are missing, and §4.1 attributing the verl margin to "DAPO optimization" is odd, since both systems ran DAPO.

## Relation to other systems

- **HybridFlow / verl (EuroSys 2025, arXiv 2409.19256).** Same workload, opposite emphasis. HybridFlow mixes single- and multi-controller execution behind hierarchical APIs that decouple computation from data dependencies, and adds a 3D-HybridEngine that reshards the actor between training and generation with claimed zero memory redundancy — precisely the transition OpenRLHF leaves as "AutoTP/AutoPP". It reports 1.53×–20.57× over baselines. OpenRLHF's counter is operational, not architectural: 1.22×–1.68× against verl v0.4.0 at 1.5B–14B with ~4× less core code.
- **DeepSpeed-Chat (arXiv 2308.01320).** What OpenRLHF was built to replace: an InstructGPT-replicating pipeline fusing training and inference optimizations in one engine, no separate inference server. Smallest codebase in Fig. 2 and the slowest baseline measured (3.6×) — the case for delegating generation to vLLM.
- **RLHFuse (NSDI 2025, arXiv 2409.13221).** Attacks what OpenRLHF leaves on the table: it blames low utilization on generation-stage data skew and training-stage pipeline bubbles, and fuses sample-level and micro-batch-level subtasks inter- and intra-stage for up to 3.7× throughput. OpenRLHF's answer to the same long tail is coarser (async queues, partial rollout) and unevaluated.
- **StreamRL (arXiv 2025, 2504.15930).** Disputes the colocated default: resource coupling limits scalability and cost-efficiency at scale, so it disaggregates with stream generation plus an output-length ranker and skewness-aware dispatch, for up to 2.66× throughput and 1.33× better cost-effectiveness in heterogeneous/cross-datacenter settings. OpenRLHF supports the disaggregated shape but recommends and measures the colocated one.
- **AReaL (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2505.24298).** Makes OpenRLHF's optional async mode the whole design: generation fully decoupled from training, rollout workers running continuously, staleness-controlled scheduling and a PPO variant tolerant of stale samples, up to 2.77× over synchronous systems at equal GPU count. Staleness is first-class there; here it is a knob with a caveat and no measurement.

## Takeaways for practice

- Role-based placement under a generic scheduler (Ray) is enough for a working, extensible RLHF cluster; weight resharding between training and inference layouts and long-tail rollout scheduling stay hard, and placement alone solves neither.
- Generate with an inference engine, never the training engine. The two largest measured wins (3.6× over DSChat, ≈3.1× over TRL) come from that one substitution.
- Make colocated-vs-disaggregated and sync-vs-async *configuration* rather than architecture, and expose the off-policy knobs (queue depth, partial rollout, IS correction) explicitly — users will trade convergence for throughput, so make the trade visible.
- Budget engineering for the weight-sync path: NCCL vs CUDA IPC, sleeping engine memory in colocated mode, and whether in-flight sequences may straddle weight versions decide both throughput and on-policyness.
- Glue-code size is a real adoption metric: ~8.5K LOC against verl's ~32K is much of why this stack ended up in teaching material and in many derivative projects. And recompute headline speedups from raw tables — here the per-step timings are internally consistent but the reported averages are not reproducible from them.

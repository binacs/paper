# Understanding Stragglers in Large Model Training Using What-if Analysis

> **Why it matters:** A five-month production trace shows that stragglers in LLM pretraining are common and mostly self-inflicted (pipeline stage imbalance, sequence-length skew, Python GC) rather than caused by bad machines, and it gives a trace-replay method for measuring how much each cause costs.

## Problem

Hybrid-parallel LLM training (DP/ZeRO, PP, TP, CP) synchronizes constantly, so one slow worker stalls the job; a slow TP worker creates a PP bubble that delays its DP group (§2.1, Fig. 1). Operators lacked answers to: how much faster would a job be without stragglers, and which workers or op types are to blame? Critical-path analysis misleads because homogeneous workloads have many near-critical paths (§2.2), and data-parallel-era fixes (backup workers, async SGD, dropped updates) fit poorly or change semantics (§1, §9). The paper is a characterization study, not a new mitigation system.

## Core design

### Trace (§3.1)
- Pretraining jobs (≥128 GPUs) on a dedicated ByteDance cluster, 2024-01-01 to 2024-05-31; dense and MoE, short and long context, on customized Megatron-LM. 8-GPU DGX-like servers; the overprovisioned three-tier CLOS leads the authors to rule out network contention.
- NDTimeline profiles 10% of steps, recording step, microbatch, PP rank, DP rank and timestamps for forward/backward compute, PP send/recv, and DP params-sync (all-gather) / grads-sync (reduce-scatter) (Table 1).

### What-if simulator (§3.2)
- **OpDuration tensors**: one 4-D tensor per op type, [step, microbatch, PP rank, DP rank]. Compute durations come from the trace; communication uses *transfer duration* = end time minus the latest start among peers in the collective/P2P pair, stripping blocking time.
- **Idealization**: comparable ops should take equal time without stragglers. Compute gets the group **mean**; communication the **median** (switched from mean because NIC/switch flapping yields extreme outliers).
- **Dependency model (Fig. 2)**: in-stream ordering; recv → consuming compute → send on a rank; params-sync → compute across DP ranks. Replaying the graph with substituted durations yields a simulated JCT.
- **Partial fixing**: to blame a slice (worker, rank, op type), idealize everything *except* it, e.g. keep OpDuration[:,:,p,d] original. DP and PP ranks are scored separately (DP+PP instead of DP×PP simulations); a worker's score is the min of its two rank scores (§5.1).

### Metrics (§3.3, §5)
- Slowdown S = T / T_ideal, where T is the simulated original timeline (Eq. 1). Per-op-type slowdown S_t = T^{-t}_ideal / T_ideal, where only type t keeps its original durations (Eq. 2). Resource waste = 1 − 1/S (Eq. 3).
- A job is straggling if S > 1.1 (§4.2). §5 writes S ≥ 1.1.
- M_W is the fraction of slowdown recovered by fixing only the slowest 3% of workers (Eq. 5). M_S = (T − T^{lastStage}_ideal)/(T − T_ideal) is the share of slowdown attributable to the last PP stage (§5.2).
- Traces whose simulated original timeline differs from the measured step time by more than 5% are dropped (§3.3, §6).

### Root causes (§5)
- **Worker/machine issues**: rare but severe (§5.1).
- **Stage partitioning imbalance**: the last PP stage also holds the loss/logit layer, far costlier than a transformer layer and worse with larger vocabularies; "ε fewer layers" tuning is fiddly (§5.2).
- **Sequence-length imbalance**: microbatch cost tracks Σlen² (Fig. 9). Detected via forward/backward compute Pearson correlation ≥0.9 on a stage. Fix: after batching, greedily repartition sequences across DP ranks with a linear cost model (multiway number partitioning), then balance microbatches per rank (§5.3).
- **Python automatic GC**: unsynchronized pauses of hundreds of ms stall the job. Fix: *planned GC*, disabling auto GC and collecting on all workers at a fixed step interval (§5.4).
- **Others**: CUDA allocator fragmentation; a false kernel dependency where MoE reduce-scatter blocks unrelated kernels, eased by raising CUDA_DEVICE_MAX_CONNECTIONS (§5.5).

### SMon (§8)
SMon runs after each NDTimeline session and shows job, per-step and per-worker slowdown, including a Pingmesh-style DP × PP heatmap. Worker faults, stage imbalance and sequence-length variance leave distinct signatures (Fig. 14). On-call is alerted when important jobs slow, matches the pattern, then drills into per-step views.

## Limitations

- **TP/CP stragglers are invisible.** NDTimeline traces at microbatch granularity, so a slow TP or CP member appears as a slow microbatch. If every microbatch is slow, the simulator has no straggler-free baseline (§7).
- **Heavy filtering**: jobs restarted >15 times (13.9%), unsimulatable jobs (50.0% of the rest) and >5%-error traces (11.2%) are dropped; the authors say this may *underestimate* prevalence and severity (§7).
- *(Own analysis)* Mean/median idealization counts all imbalance as straggling, so T_ideal is an attribution baseline, not necessarily an achievable target (equal stage costs may be unpartitionable).
- **CPU-side gaps**: data loading, padding and launch delays are untraced and are the main simulator error (§6). *(Own analysis)* Host-side and input-pipeline stragglers are therefore under-counted by construction.
- **Prototype mitigations**: sequence redistribution tested on one job, not deployed at scale, PP-level imbalance unsolved (§5.3); planned GC needs per-job interval tuning (OOM risk) and is off by default, with no GC prevalence figure (§5.4).
- *(Own analysis)* Dedicated, overprovisioned cluster with no expert parallelism in analyzed jobs (§2.1, §3.1); shared clusters may see more communication/contention stragglers, as FALCON reports (§9).

## Relation to other systems

- **MegaScale (NSDI 2024)**: abstract describes full-stack co-design at >10,000 GPUs (55.2% MFU, 175B model, 12,288 GPUs; 1.34× over Megatron-LM) plus diagnostic tools to find root causes and mitigate stragglers. This paper is the fleet-wide quantitative counterpart: how much stragglers cost and why, not a new training system.
- **ByteRobust (arXiv 2509.16293)**: abstract centers on detecting and recovering from *failures* (CUDA errors, NaNs, hangs) with fast fault localization; 97% ETTR on a three-month 9,600-GPU job. That is fail-stop/hang; this paper is fail-slow in running jobs, mostly from workload imbalance rather than faulty machines.
- **Llama 3 (arXiv 2024)**: abstract covers only the models (405B dense Transformer, 128K context, multimodal extensions), not infrastructure; this paper's §9 cites it among infrastructure reports that mention stragglers without in-depth analysis.
- **Acme (NSDI 2024)**: abstract characterizes a six-month LLM-development trace (utilization, failures vs. older DL workloads) and adds LLM-assisted failure diagnosis/recovery and decoupled evaluation scheduling. Both are trace studies; Acme spans failures and scheduling across the lifecycle, while this paper replays per-op timelines to cost slowness inside pretraining jobs.
- **GEMINI (SOSP 2023)**: abstract describes in-CPU-memory checkpoints with near-optimal placement and traffic scheduling, >13× faster recovery, per-iteration checkpointing at no throughput cost. GEMINI shortens recovery after failures; this paper shows sizable GPU-hour loss from jobs that never fail, which checkpoint/recovery cannot address.

## Takeaways for practice

- Sample-profile steps with step/microbatch/rank IDs and keep a what-if replayer: it turns "slow job" into slowdown and waste attributed by op type and rank.
- Idealize compute by mean, communication by median, and measure communication as transfer time so blocking is not blamed on the network.
- Check the last PP stage (loss/logits, large vocab) and the sequence-length mix before hunting bad nodes; in aggregate, imbalance costs more than hardware.
- Pack sequences and assign DP ranks by a Σlen²-like cost, not token count.
- Disable automatic Python GC in large synchronous jobs; collect on a synchronized step schedule with an OOM guard.
- A DP × PP slowdown heatmap is cheap on-call triage: a hot cell, a hot row and diffuse noise point to different causes.
- Persistent slowdown (per-step ≈ job slowdown) implies static/config causes; fix at launch rather than with runtime backup workers.

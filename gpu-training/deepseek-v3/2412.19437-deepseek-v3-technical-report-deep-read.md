# DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report

> **Why it matters:** A 671B-total / 37B-active MoE model trained on 2048 H800 GPUs without tensor parallelism, by co-designing the pipeline schedule, the all-to-all kernels, FP8 numerics and the routing constraints around the hardware's NVLink/IB bandwidth ratio.

*Read from arXiv v2 (18 Feb 2025), PDF text, Sections 1–4.5. Sections 5–6 and the appendices were not readable in the fetched text and are not used here.*

## Problem

Training and serving a very large fine-grained MoE model on a fixed H800 GPU cluster where cross-node expert-parallel all-to-all traffic is expensive. Without intervention, the paper says cross-node EP leads to a computation-to-communication ratio of about 1:1 (Sec. 3.2.1). Goals: hide that communication, avoid TP, fit in memory, train in FP8 without accuracy loss, and balance experts without a quality-hurting auxiliary loss (Sec. 2.1.2, 3).

## Core design

### Model features that shape the system
- MLA attention and DeepSeekMoE from DeepSeek-V2; each MoE layer has 1 shared and 256 routed experts, 8 routed experts active per token (Sec. 4.2).
- **Auxiliary-loss-free balancing:** sigmoid affinity scores; a per-expert bias is added to scores *only for top-k selection* and is lowered for overloaded / raised for underloaded experts at the end of each step by speed γ. A small sequence-wise balance loss remains as a safeguard (Sec. 2.1.2).
- **Node-limited routing:** each token goes to at most M nodes (M = 4 in practice), which bounds cross-node traffic. No token dropping in training or inference (Sec. 2.1.2, 4.2).
- **Multi-token prediction (MTP):** sequential MTP modules keep the full causal chain and share the embedding and output head with the main model; they can be discarded at inference or reused for speculative decoding (Sec. 2.2).

### DualPipe (in the HAI-LLM framework; PP16, EP64, ZeRO-1 DP, no TP, Sec. 3.2)
- Each forward chunk is split into attention, all-to-all dispatch, MLP, all-to-all combine (plus a PP communication component); backward attention and MLP are further split into input-gradient and weight-gradient parts (Sec. 3.2.1).
- A forward and a backward chunk are paired and rearranged so that communication of one overlaps compute of the other, with the SM split between communication and computation tuned manually. The paper states both all-to-all and PP communication "can be fully hidden" (Sec. 3.2.1).
- Bidirectional schedule: micro-batches enter from both ends of the pipeline. Cost: two copies of parameters, which the paper says is acceptable because of the large EP size; requirement only that PP stages and micro-batches are divisible by 2; bubbles and activation memory do not grow with micro-batch count (Sec. 3.2.1, Table 2).

### Cross-node all-to-all kernels
- Tokens go over IB to the GPU with the same in-node index on each target node, then over NVLink to the GPU holding the expert; IB and NVLink transfers are fully overlapped (Sec. 3.2.2).
- 20 SMs split into 10 communication channels; warp specialization with separate warps for IB send, IB-to-NVLink forward, NVLink receive (dispatch) and the reverse plus accumulation (combine); warp counts adjust dynamically; customized PTX and auto-tuned chunk sizes reduce L2 cache use and interference with compute SMs (Sec. 3.2.2).

### Memory saving
Recompute all RMSNorm ops and MLA up-projections in backward; keep the parameter EMA in CPU memory, updated asynchronously; place the shallowest layers (embedding) and deepest layers (output head) on the same PP rank so MTP and the main model physically share those parameters and gradients (Sec. 3.2.3).

### FP8 training
- All three Linear GEMMs (Fprop, Dgrad, Wgrad) in FP8; embedding, output head, MoE gating, normalization and attention stay in BF16/FP32; master weights, weight gradients and optimizer states kept in higher precision (Sec. 3.3.1).
- Fine-grained scaling: 1x128 tiles for activations (per token per 128 channels), 128x128 blocks for weights; scales computed online from max-abs; E4M3 for all tensors instead of an E4M3/E5M2 hybrid (Sec. 3.3.2).
- H800 FP8 GEMM accumulation retains only about 14 bits; partial sums are promoted to FP32 on CUDA cores every N_C = 128 elements (4 WGMMAs), with two warpgroups overlapping promotion and MMA (Sec. 3.3.2).
- AdamW moments in BF16; master weights and accumulated gradients in FP32; activations cached in FP8; SwiGLU inputs cached and output recomputed; MoE dispatch quantized to FP8 while combine stays BF16 (Sec. 3.3.3).

### Inference and deployment (prefill/decode separated)
- **Prefill:** minimum unit 4 nodes / 32 GPUs; attention TP4 + SP with DP8; MoE EP32; 32 redundant experts chosen from observed load and re-chosen periodically (e.g., every 10 minutes); each GPU hosts its original 8 experts plus one redundant; two micro-batches of similar load overlapped (Sec. 3.4.1).
- **Decode:** shared expert treated as a routed one (9 experts selected per token); minimum unit 40 nodes / 320 GPUs; attention TP4 + SP with DP80; MoE EP320; one expert per GPU, 64 GPUs host redundant and shared experts; point-to-point IB all-to-all with IBGDA; only a small share of SMs given to dispatch+MoE+combine (Sec. 3.4.2).

### Hardware suggestions
Offload communication from SMs (co-processor; NVIDIA SHARP named) and unify IB and NVLink behind one interface (Sec. 3.5.1); higher FP8 accumulation precision; native tile/block-wise scaling in Tensor Cores; fused FP8 cast with TMA; near-memory BF16-to-FP8 cast; direct transposed reads for GEMM (Sec. 3.5.2).

## Limitations

- The training-cost figure explicitly excludes prior research and ablation experiments on architectures, algorithms or data (Sec. 1); it is a rental-price estimate, not total spend.
- Serving needs large units: 320 GPUs minimum for decode (Sec. 3.4.2). (Own analysis) This favours large providers; small deployments cannot reproduce the reported layout.
- DualPipe keeps two parameter copies, justified by large EP (Sec. 3.2.1). (Own analysis) Dense or small-EP models would pay more for this.
- Redundant-expert placement is periodic and statistics-driven; dynamic redundancy is only being explored (Sec. 3.4.1, 3.4.2).
- The FP8 recipe relies on CUDA-core promotion and custom formats to work around Hopper's ~14-bit accumulation (Sec. 3.3.2, 3.5.2). (Own analysis) Its overhead and portability to other accelerators are not quantified in the sections read.
- (Own analysis) The node limit and 20-SM channel layout are tuned to the H800's NVLink/IB ratio; other interconnects would need retuning.

## Relation to other systems

- **1F1B and ZB1P (zero-bubble PP):** (Own analysis) DualPipe reuses the input/weight backward split of zero-bubble PP but adds forward/backward chunk overlap and a bidirectional schedule; Table 2 compares bubbles and memory directly (Sec. 3.2.1).
- **Chimera:** also bidirectional; the paper says DualPipe only requires PP stages and micro-batches divisible by 2 (Sec. 3.2.1).
- **GShard-style expert parallelism:** cited for EP (Sec. 3.2); DeepSeek-V3 adds node-limited routing and auxiliary-loss-free balancing instead of relying on an auxiliary loss (Sec. 2.1.2).
- **DeepSeek-V2:** source of MLA and DeepSeekMoE; FP8 validation was done at V2-Lite/V2-like scales (Sec. 2.1, 3.3).
- **Hybrid FP8 recipes in existing FP8 frameworks (NVIDIA, cited):** use E5M2 in Dgrad and Wgrad; DeepSeek-V3 uses E4M3 everywhere with tile/block scaling and FP32 promotion (Sec. 3.3.2); the paper notes current GPUs support only per-tensor quantization natively (Sec. 3.5.2).

## Takeaways for practice

- Size routing constraints to the interconnect: the node limit was chosen from the NVLink/IB bandwidth ratio so that each IB transfer feeds several experts per node (Sec. 3.2.2).
- Communication is not free even when "overlapped": it cost 20 of 132 SMs (Sec. 3.5.1). Schedulers and profilers should count SMs spent on collectives as lost compute.
- Pipeline schedules can be co-designed with MoE all-to-all; splitting chunks into attention/dispatch/MLP/combine gives the overlap unit (Sec. 3.2.1).
- FP8 training at scale needed fine-grained scaling and periodic FP32 promotion, not just per-tensor casts (Sec. 3.3.2).
- Serving: separate prefill and decode, use different parallelism per phase, and rebalance hot experts with redundancy on a periodic timer (Sec. 3.4). (Own analysis) A cluster scheduler for such workloads must place whole multi-node units (32 or 320 GPUs) with IB locality.

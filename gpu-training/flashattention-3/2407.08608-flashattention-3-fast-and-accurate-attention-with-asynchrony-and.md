# FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision

> **Why it matters:** On Hopper the win no longer comes from saving HBM traffic but from scheduling asynchronous units (TMA, WGMMA) against each other and making FP8 numerically survivable — a pattern that transfers to any fused kernel.

## Problem

FlashAttention-2 targets Ampere, where the tensor-core matmul is synchronous and the kernel's job is mainly to avoid materialising S and P in HBM (§2.3). On Hopper that leaves most of the machine idle: the paper measures FlashAttention-2 at about **35% of peak** on an H100 while tuned GEMM kernels reach **80–85%** (§1). Three hardware facts cause the gap (§2.2): the Tensor Memory Accelerator (TMA) does global-to-shared copies asynchronously; WGMMA is a warpgroup-wide *asynchronous* matmul reading operands straight from shared memory; FP8 tensor cores give 2× the per-SM throughput of FP16/BF16. Meanwhile `exp` does not get faster — FP16 matmul is ~989 TFLOPS against ~3.9 TFLOPS for exponentials on H100 SXM5 (§3.1), so softmax is a serial tail taking up to roughly half the cycle at head dim 128, worse in FP8. Attention on Hopper is therefore a *scheduling* problem plus a *numerics* problem.

## Core design

### 1. Producer–consumer warp specialisation over TMA + WGMMA (§3.1, Alg. 1)

Parallelisation is unchanged from FlashAttention-2 (batch × heads × query blocks; one CTA owns a query tile `Q_i` of `B_r` rows and loops over `T_c` key/value tiles of `B_c` rows). What changes is the division of labour inside the CTA. A **producer warpgroup** calls `setmaxnreg` to give its registers away, then issues TMA loads of `Q_i` and each `K_j`, `V_j` into an *s*-stage circular shared-memory buffer, running ahead without waiting for the first *s* iterations. **Consumer warpgroups** reclaim those registers and run the mainloop: `S = Q_i K_jᵀ` as an SS-WGMMA (both operands in SMEM), the online-softmax update of running max `m_i` and sum `ℓ_i`, then `O_i += P̃ V_j` as an RS-WGMMA (first operand in registers); finally `O_i` is normalised and `L_i = m_i + log ℓ_i` written out. Because TMA has its own hardware queue, memory and issue latency are hidden by whichever warpgroup is not waiting. MQA/GQA are index arithmetic, so K and V are never duplicated in HBM.

### 2. Ping-pong scheduling and intra-warpgroup GEMM–softmax overlap (§3.1–3.2)

*Ping-pong* works **between** two consumer warpgroups: `bar.sync` barriers stagger them so one warpgroup's softmax runs while the other's WGMMAs hold the tensor cores, then the roles swap. This alone moves FP16 forward (head dim 128, seqlen 8192) from 570 to 620–640 TFLOPS, though the paper notes the real schedule is "not as clean as depicted".

*Two-stage pipelining* works **inside** one warpgroup (§3.2, Alg. 2). Within an iteration the softmax depends on `S_i^(j)` and the second GEMM on `P̃_i^(j)`, so the waits serialise everything. FlashAttention-3 breaks the dependency across iterations with an extra `S_next` register buffer (`B_r × B_c × sizeof(float)` per CTA), overlapping iteration *j*'s softmax with iteration *j+1*'s first WGMMA; the cost is register pressure, which bounds tile size. A SASS check (App. B.2) shows the compiler interleaves softmax only with the *first* WGMMA, and a 3-stage variant (App. B.3) was slower because of the extra registers.

### 3. FP8: layout conformance, block quantisation, incoherent processing (§3.3, App. B.7–B.8)

**Layout.** FP8 WGMMA accepts only k-major SMEM operands, and the first GEMM's FP32 accumulator layout does not match the operand-A layout the second wants. Rather than transposing V in global memory, the producer transposes each V tile *in shared memory* via LDSM/STSM through registers, with `__byte_perm` fix-ups because those instructions cannot split two 8-bit values packed in a 16-bit slot. The accumulator→operand conversion permutes P's columns (byte permutes plus `__shfl_sync` over fixed maps), and the V transpose deliberately emits a *matching row permutation*, exploiting `P·V = colperm(P)·rowperm(V)`.

**Accuracy.** Instead of one scale per tensor, Q/K/V are quantised per block of `B_r × d` or `B_c × d`; the scales fold into the rescaling of S the algorithm already does, so they are free, and quantisation can fuse into a preceding bandwidth-bound op such as RoPE. On top, *incoherent processing* (from QuIP/QuIP#) multiplies Q and K by a random orthogonal `M` — random ±1 diagonals composed with a Hadamard matrix — before quantisation: `MMᵀ = I` leaves `QKᵀ` unchanged but smears outliers across the head dimension, at `O(d log d)` cost and also fusable with RoPE.

### Forward vs backward

The forward pass gets all three. The **backward pass** (App. B.1, Alg. 3) reuses only warp specialisation, plus a fourth role: a dedicated **dQ-writer warp**. A preprocessing kernel computes `D = rowsum(dO ∘ O)`; the producer streams `K_j`, `V_j` once and then `Q_i`, `dO_i`; consumers keep `dK_j`, `dV_j` in registers across query blocks, computing `S` and `dP`, then `dS = P ∘ (dP − D_i)` elementwise, `dV_j += PᵀdO_i`, `dK_j += dSᵀQ_i` and `dQ_local = dS K_j`. Since many CTAs accumulate into the same `dQ` rows, `dQ_local` goes to SMEM and the dQ-writer warp performs the semaphore-guarded atomic add, so matmul warps never block on that contention. The paper never says ping-pong or two-stage pipelining is applied in the backward pass, and **FP8 is measured for the forward pass only** (Fig. 7, App. C.2); backward benchmarks are BF16.

## Limitations

- **FP8 is forward-only and unproven in training.** §5 lists "understanding the effects of low-precision attention in large-scale training" as future work; the evidence is RMSE against an FP64 reference on synthetic Gaussian inputs with injected outliers, with no end-to-end quality result.
- **The accuracy win is almost entirely incoherent processing.** Removing block quantisation moves RMSE 9.1e-3 → 9.3e-3; removing the Hadamard transform returns it to the 2.4e-2 baseline. *(My reading of Table 3; the paper reports the rows without drawing this conclusion.)*
- **Causal masking and short sequences are the weak spot.** arXiv v2's footnote puts FP8 FA3 ahead of cuDNN at head dim 64, on par at 128/256 un-masked, and *behind with causal masking*, blaming the lack of a persistent kernel and load balancing. The NeurIPS version adds a persistent kernel (App. B.6) and drops that footnote without a per-head-dim cuDNN breakdown, so the position is never re-established. *(Cross-version comparison is mine.)*
- **Hopper-specific and CUTLASS-deep**, assuming `setmaxnreg`, TMA, WGMMA and warpgroup barriers, with an FP8 path tied to exact LDSM/STSM layouts and hard-coded shuffle maps; the generality footnote ("any GPU with robust asynchrony and low-precision support") is asserted, not shown. *(Mine.)*
- **Register pressure caps the overlap**: the 3-stage variant regressed (App. B.3).
- **Not reproducible from the paper alone** — tile sizes, pipeline depth *s*, `setmaxnreg` counts and the FP8 kernel's overlap mechanism are never stated, and headline numbers changed between arXiv v2 and the NeurIPS version with no erratum. *(Mine.)*

## Relation to other systems

- **FlashAttention (NeurIPS 2022)** introduced the IO-aware tiled single-kernel formulation with online-softmax rescaling — the invariant FA3 still computes (§2.3) — optimising HBM traffic. On Hopper that is no longer the binding constraint, which is why FA3 is a scheduling paper rather than an IO paper.
- **FlashAttention-2 (ICLR 2024)** added sequence-dimension parallelism and better work partitioning (§1). FA3 keeps that decomposition and replaces the *intra-CTA* execution model: synchronous MMA → async WGMMA, manual loads → TMA, uniform warps → producer/consumer roles, serial softmax → ping-pong plus two-stage pipelining. Table 2 shows the two scheduling mechanisms are complementary (661 vs 582/570 TFLOPs/s when either is removed); numerically FA3 only matches FA2 in FP16 (both 1.9e-4).
- **cuDNN 9**, the closed-source vendor kernel (itself a Hopper-tuned FlashAttention-2), is the bar to clear: §4.1 reports FA3 surpassing it for sequences of 1k and above, with the FP8/causal caveat above. The transferable claim is that an open CUTLASS-level kernel can match a vendor library on the vendor's newest instructions.
- **FlashAttention-2 in Triton**, the compiler-based baseline that also emits Hopper instructions, trails both FA3 and cuDNN in the FP8 figures — the paper's implicit argument that hand-written warp specialisation and in-kernel layout surgery still beat what a tile-level DSL expresses. **ThunderKittens** is cited in §1 as another tile-abstraction effort but not benchmarked.
- **QuIP / QuIP#** are the source of incoherent processing, developed there for weight quantisation; FA3 moves the random-orthogonal trick into the *activation* path of a fused kernel. Other low-precision attention work cited targets KV-cache compression for inference, not the GEMMs.
- **DeepSeek-V3 (DeepSeek-AI technical report, arXiv 2412.19437)** is the complementary training-scale point: an FP8 mixed-precision framework for a 671B-parameter MoE (37B activated) on 14.8T tokens for ~2.788M H800 GPU-hours, relative loss error below 0.25% versus BF16, activations cached and dispatched in FP8 (its §3.3) — but **attention operators stay in BF16/FP32** (§3.3.1). The two bracket FA3's open question: FP8 is validated end-to-end for dense pre-training matmuls, while FP8 attention remained a forward-pass kernel large-scale trainers had not adopted.

## Takeaways for practice

- On accelerators with independent async engines, throughput comes from **role specialisation plus pipelining**, not from cutting memory traffic further. The producer/consumer + circular-buffer pattern — a TMA warp, MMA warps, a writer warp for contended atomics — generalises to any fused kernel contending on global memory in its epilogue.
- **Find the unit that did not get faster.** Matmul throughput doubles per precision step while `exp` does not; measure the serial fraction (570 → 640 TFLOPS from scheduling alone) before touching data movement.
- **Low precision is a layout problem as much as a numerics problem**: half the FP8 work here reconciles k-major operand rules, accumulator layouts and packed-byte transposes.
- **Match quantisation granularity to the kernel's existing blocking** (free here), and try a **Hadamard-based rotation** fused into a preceding bandwidth-bound op: it bought far more accuracy than finer scales.
- **Do not assume the published peak applies to your shape.** Headline figures are non-causal, long-sequence, large-head-dim; causal masking, short sequences and ragged batches each cost separately, so benchmark the masked, variable-length case you serve, with pinned clocks and library versions.

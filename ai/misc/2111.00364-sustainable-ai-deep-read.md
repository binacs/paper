# Sustainable AI: Environmental Implications, Challenges and Opportunities

> **Why it matters:** It is an end-to-end carbon accounting of production ML at Facebook, covering data, experimentation, training and inference as well as manufacturing (embodied) carbon. It argues that efficiency and utilization work alone cannot keep up with AI growth.

Version read: arXiv v2 (9 Jan 2022). The arXiv text names no venue for the paper itself. The venue and year above come from the task.

## Problem

The paper argues that AI demand is growing super-linearly along three axes at once: data, model size and infrastructure (§I, §VI). Earlier carbon studies mostly looked at the operational cost of training one large model. The authors widen the scope in three ways:

- the whole development cycle: Data Processing, Experimentation, Training and Inference (§II-A);
- the hardware life cycle, especially manufacturing ("embodied carbon") next to operational carbon (§II-B);
- fleet-level effects, where efficiency gains are partly cancelled by growth in demand. The paper calls this Jevon's Paradox (§III-B, Fig. 8).

The central question is how much of AI's footprint optimization can remove, and what remains once operational energy is clean and efficient.

## Core design

This is an analysis and position paper, not a system design. Its "mechanisms" are an accounting method plus a set of optimization levers.

### Accounting method (§III-A)

- **Workloads:** six production models. RM1–RM5 are "five unique deep learning recommendation and ranking models for various Facebook products". LM is "Facebook's Transformer-based Universal Language Model for text translation". The paper compares them with seven open-source large models, including Meena, GPT-3 and Switch Transformer.
- **Operational carbon:** measured energy for each phase, location-based carbon intensity, and a PUE of 1.1. Renewable procurement is discussed, but the plotted figures are location-based.
- **Embodied carbon:** a life cycle analysis restricted to manufacturing. A GPU training server is assumed to have about the manufacturing footprint of an Apple 28-core CPU system with dual AMD Radeon GPUs, and CPU-only systems half of that. The amortization assumes 30–60% average utilization over a 3- to 5-year server lifetime.
- **Phase split:** Fig. 3 breaks down power capacity and RM1 energy by phase. It shows that inference and data pipelines are not negligible next to training.

### Optimization levers

- **Cross-stack co-design for LM (§III-B, Fig. 7).** The gains come in stepwise "additional" stages:
  - platform-level (application) caching;
  - GPU-based serving;
  - algorithmic changes, namely halving precision from 32-bit to 16-bit and custom operators that schedule encoding steps.
- **RM memory efficiency (§III-B).** Embedding tables dominate RM model size. FP32→FP16 embeddings shrink the model, cut memory bandwidth and lower inference latency. TT-Rec and DHE are named as memory-efficient embedding alternatives (§IV-B).
- **Fleet-level iteration (§III-B, Figs. 6, 8).** The fleet's power footprint is reduced in repeated 6-month cycles. Because demand keeps growing, the net two-year reduction is far below the per-cycle rate.
- **Infrastructure at scale (§III-C).** The levers are denser accelerators, custom server SKUs, low PUE, idle-power management, and Twine auto-scaling, which frees off-peak web-tier machines for offline training.
- **Utilization and clean energy (§III-D, Fig. 9).** Higher GPU utilization amortizes embodied carbon. Carbon-free energy then removes most of the operational part, which leaves embodied carbon as the dominant term.
- **Sustainability mindset (§IV).** The paper groups its proposals by stage:
  - Data: sampling and data "perishability", meaning old data loses value.
  - Experimentation: sample-efficient NAS/HPO (Bayesian optimization, early stopping) and energy as an explicit objective.
  - Training: communication-efficient training.
  - Infrastructure: multi-tenancy and virtualization, carbon-aware scheduling, and on-device or federated learning (Fig. 11).
- **Telemetry (§V-A).** The paper asks for easy carbon measurement and for reporting norms: operational and embodied carbon, hardware, machine count and runtime, in both papers and model cards.

## Limitations

- **Internal inconsistencies.** The LM reduction is "more than 800×" in §III-B and Fig. 7 but "810×" in §VI. The two-year fleet reduction is 28.5% in §III-B and Fig. 8 but 25.8% in §VI.
- **Coarse embodied model.** One workstation footprint stands in for GPU servers, with a fixed halving for CPU servers. The paper does not show how the utilization assumption enters the amortization.
- **Single operator.** All production numbers come from one fleet at PUE of about 1.1. (Own analysis: results may not transfer to fleets with a different PUE or grid mix.)
- **Accounting basis.** The plots are location-based; renewable matching is discussed but not netted into them.
- **Measurement detail.** The paper does not describe how per-phase energy was metered.
- **Federated learning estimate.** It rests on assumed constant device and router power (App. B).
- **No new system.** Most levers are cited prior work. The contribution is measurement and framing.

## Relation to other systems

- **Meena, GPT-3, Switch Transformer.** Their published training footprints are reference points. The paper's claim is that production models, measured across the life cycle including inference, exceed Meena on average (§III-A).
- **Strubell et al., Lacoste et al., CodeCarbon.** These measure or estimate operational emissions. This paper adds embodied carbon and the experimentation and inference phases. (Own analysis: complementary scope, not a competing tool.)
- **Twine.** It is named as the auto-scaler whose off-peak capacity can host offline training (§III-C).
- **TT-Rec, DHE.** Embedding compression is presented as a carbon lever via lower memory capacity and better accelerator use (§IV-B).
- **GPipe, ZeRO/DeepSpeed, QSGD, PowerSGD.** These are framed as techniques that enable larger models. (Own analysis: the paper's Jevons argument implies they do not by themselves lower the total footprint.)
- **Carbon-aware computing (Radovanovic et al.).** The paper names time-shifting to clean energy and notes that it may need over-provisioning, which adds embodied carbon (§IV-C).

## Takeaways for practice

(Own analysis, for people building GPU training and inference systems.)

- **Budget for inference and the data pipeline, not just training.** The 10:20:70 capacity split and RM1's 31:29:40 energy split put serving and ingestion on par with training.
- **Treat utilization as a carbon metric.** Experimentation GPUs run at 30–50%, and 80% utilization gave a 3× drop. Packing, multi-tenancy and reuse of idle capacity are first-order levers.
- **Stack serving optimizations and measure each step.** In the LM chain, caching, GPU serving, lower precision and custom kernels each contribute separately.
- **Use low precision where memory dominates.** For embedding-heavy recommenders, FP16 cut size and bandwidth and improved latency.
- **Decouple data ingestion from trainers.** This keeps GPUs from waiting on input.
- **Plan for Jevons.** Per-cycle efficiency gains did not translate one-for-one into net reduction, so model demand growth, not just per-job savings.
- **Put energy and hardware into job telemetry.** Recording GPU count, runtime and energy per job lets operational and embodied cost be attributed.
- **After clean energy, accelerator count and lifetime dominate.** Fewer, better-utilized accelerators matter more than marginal operational savings.

# What is the State of Neural Network Pruning?

## TL;DR

A meta-analysis of neural network pruning literature across 81 papers and hundreds of controlled model prunings, finding the field lacks standardized benchmarks and metrics, and introducing ShrinkBench, an open-source framework for standardized pruning evaluation.

## Problem & Motivation

Neural network pruning has been studied for three decades, but the literature is fragmented. Papers use inconsistent benchmarks, metrics, and experimental setups, making it hard to compare pruning techniques against one another or to gauge how much real progress has been made.

The authors argue this lack of standardization is severe enough that reported results across papers are largely incomparable, obscuring which methods actually work and under what conditions.

## Key Ideas

- Meta-analysis aggregating results across 81 pruning papers to survey approaches and identify consistent findings.
- Controlled re-pruning of hundreds of models to test claims under common conditions.
- Central finding: the community lacks standardized benchmarks and metrics, undermining comparability.
- Identification of specific problematic practices in how pruning is evaluated and reported.
- Concrete proposed remedies for evaluation and reporting.
- ShrinkBench: an open-source framework enabling standardized evaluation of pruning methods.
- Demonstration that comprehensive, standardized evaluation exposes pitfalls in naive method comparisons.

## System Design

- ShrinkBench is an open-source software framework that standardizes the evaluation pipeline for pruning methods.
- It provides a common harness so different pruning techniques can be run and compared under identical conditions (same models, datasets, metrics).
- The framework supports comprehensive evaluation, surfacing issues that arise when comparing pruning methods naively.
- The paper pairs the framework with a meta-analysis component: aggregating published results and re-running prunings in controlled settings.

## Evaluation

- Aggregated results from 81 papers and pruned hundreds of models under controlled conditions.
- Used ShrinkBench to compare various pruning techniques.
- Showed that comprehensive standardized evaluation can prevent common pitfalls when comparing pruning methods.
- Headline qualitative result: lack of standardized benchmarks/metrics makes cross-paper comparison and progress measurement difficult.

## Takeaways

- Pruning research suffers from non-standardized benchmarks and metrics, making cross-paper comparisons unreliable.
- Meta-analysis plus controlled re-pruning is a useful way to audit a subfield's claims.
- Standardized tooling (ShrinkBench) is proposed as the fix for comparability.
- Naive comparisons of pruning methods can be misleading; comprehensive evaluation matters.
- Progress over three decades is hard to establish without common evaluation protocols.

## Open Questions

- Whether the community will adopt standardized benchmarks and metrics proposed by the authors.
- How to design benchmarks that remain fair across diverse model architectures and pruning regimes.
- Limitations of a meta-analysis based on published results, which may be biased or incomplete.
- Whether ShrinkBench's evaluation scope generalizes to newer pruning paradigms beyond those surveyed.

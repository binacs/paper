# What is the State of Neural Network Pruning?

## TL;DR

A meta-analysis of 81 neural network pruning papers plus controlled re-evaluation of hundreds of models, finding the field lacks standardized benchmarks and metrics, and introducing ShrinkBench, an open-source framework for standardized pruning evaluation.

## Problem & Motivation

Neural network pruning research has proliferated, but results are reported under inconsistent experimental setups, metrics, and baselines. This makes it nearly impossible to compare pruning techniques against each other or to judge how much real progress has been made over roughly three decades.

The authors argue this is not a minor reporting nuisance but a structural deficiency: without standardized benchmarks and metrics, claimed improvements may reflect differing conditions rather than better methods.

## Key Ideas

- Aggregate results across 81 pruning papers to survey approaches and identify consistent findings.
- Re-prune hundreds of models under controlled conditions rather than trusting reported numbers.
- Diagnose specific flaws in current evaluation practice and propose concrete remedies.
- Introduce ShrinkBench, an open-source framework enabling standardized pruning evaluations.
- Demonstrate that comprehensive, standardized evaluation exposes pitfalls in naive method comparisons.

## System Design

- ShrinkBench is an open-source software framework for evaluating pruning methods under a common protocol.
- It standardizes the experimental pipeline so different pruning techniques are compared on the same models, metrics, and conditions.
- The paper pairs the framework with a meta-analysis layer: literature aggregation plus controlled re-pruning of hundreds of models.
- Evaluation output is designed to be comprehensive enough to reveal comparison pitfalls that ad hoc setups hide.

## Evaluation

- Workloads: 81 published pruning papers aggregated, plus hundreds of models pruned under controlled conditions.
- Baselines: comparison across various pruning techniques using ShrinkBench's standardized evaluation.
- Headline finding: the clearest result is the community's lack of standardized benchmarks and metrics, severe enough to hinder cross-technique comparison and progress measurement.
- The framework is shown to prevent common pitfalls that arise when comparing pruning methods.

## Takeaways

- Reported pruning gains are hard to trust without shared benchmarks, metrics, and baselines.
- Meta-analysis plus controlled re-pruning is a useful pattern for auditing a research field.
- Standardized tooling (ShrinkBench) can change conclusions by removing confounds.
- Progress in pruning over decades is difficult to quantify given current practice.
- Concrete remedies for evaluation practice are as important as new pruning algorithms.

## Open Questions

- Which specific metrics and benchmarks should become the community standard, and will adoption follow?
- How much of the historical pruning literature's claimed progress survives standardized re-evaluation?
- Does ShrinkBench's protocol generalize across architectures, tasks, and hardware-aware pruning settings?
- What incentives would push authors and reviewers toward standardized reporting?

# What is the State of Neural Network Pruning?

## TL;DR

A meta-analysis of neural network pruning literature that reveals a lack of standardized benchmarks and metrics, and introduces ShrinkBench, an open-source framework for standardized evaluation of pruning methods.

## Problem & Motivation

Neural network pruning research lacks standardized benchmarks and metrics, making it difficult to compare pruning techniques or assess progress over the past three decades. This deficiency hinders reproducibility and fair comparison across methods.

## Key Ideas

- Meta-analysis of 81 papers and hundreds of models under controlled conditions.
- Identification of inconsistent evaluation practices and metrics in pruning literature.
- Proposal of concrete remedies for standardization.
- Introduction of ShrinkBench, an open-source framework for standardized pruning evaluations.
- Demonstration that comprehensive evaluation prevents common pitfalls in comparing pruning methods.

## System Design

ShrinkBench is an open-source framework designed to facilitate standardized evaluations of pruning methods. It provides a consistent environment for benchmarking, including standardized metrics and protocols. The framework enables fair comparison by controlling for variables that often differ across studies.

## Evaluation

The paper aggregates results from 81 papers and prunes hundreds of models in controlled conditions. It uses ShrinkBench to compare various pruning techniques, showing that its comprehensive evaluation can prevent common pitfalls when comparing pruning methods.

## Takeaways

- The pruning field suffers from a lack of standardized benchmarks and metrics.
- Without standardization, it is hard to compare techniques or measure progress.
- ShrinkBench provides a standardized framework for evaluation.
- Comprehensive evaluation is necessary to avoid misleading comparisons.

## Open Questions

How to ensure widespread adoption of standardized benchmarks? What are the best metrics for evaluating pruning methods? How to extend ShrinkBench to new architectures and tasks?

# Quantifying the Rise and Fall of Complexity in Closed Systems: The Coffee Automaton

> **Why it matters:** Entropy only goes up, but "interesting structure" seems to rise and then fall. The paper turns that intuition into a measurable quantity, the compressed size of a coarse-grained state, and checks it on a toy cellular automaton. It also shows how easily the measurement apparatus itself can create fake complexity.

Version used: arXiv v1 (2014-05-27), the only version, read from the arXiv PDF.

## Problem

Pour cream into coffee. The start (two layers) and the end (uniform mix) are simple to describe, while the tendrils in between are not, yet entropy rises monotonically throughout. The paper asks whether "complexity" can be defined so that it is low at both ends and peaks in the middle, and whether it can be estimated numerically on a simple closed system (§1). The measure should rest on a formal notion (Kolmogorov complexity and relatives, §2), be approximable in practice (§4), and separate real structure from artifacts of the measurement (§6).

## Core design

### Candidate measures (§2)

The paper surveys several formal notions and picks one:

- **Apparent complexity (§2.1):** H(f(x)), where f is a smoothing function and H an entropy measure. Choosing f is arbitrary, but the paper argues that spatial smoothing is physically motivated.
- **Sophistication (§2.2), logical depth (§2.3) and light-cone complexity (§2.4)** are reviewed and set aside for the reasons given under Relation to other systems.
- **Synthesis (§2.5):** apparent complexity is treated as a resource-bounded form of sophistication. The authors adopt it, with squares of cells as the smoothing regions.

### The automaton (§3)

- State: a 2D bit array, 1 = cream and 0 = coffee. The top half starts as cream and the bottom half as coffee. The implementation is in Python, and the paper says the source is available for download from the authors.
- **Interacting model (§3.1):** at most one particle per cell. At each step, "one pair of horizontally or vertically adjacent, differing particles is selected, and the particles' positions are swapped."
- **Non-interacting model (§3.2):** any number of cream particles may share a cell, and "each cream particle in the system moves one step in a randomly chosen direction" per time step. This model was chosen because it can be analyzed.

### Estimating the quantities (§4, §5.1)

- Kolmogorov complexity is uncomputable, so it is approximated by the output size of a general-purpose compressor ("such as gzip or bzip"). Such estimates are only upper bounds (§7).
- **Entropy estimate:** compressed size of the fine-grained array.
- **Complexity estimate:** compressed size of a coarse-grained array. Each cell is replaced by the average over a g × g square centered on it (g is the grain size, "selected experimentally"). The averages are then thresholded "into three buckets" (coffee, cream, mixed).
- Two approaches were tried and dropped. The OSCR two-part-code algorithm (Evans et al.) gave noisy output on the automaton data. A diff-based two-part code that estimated K(x|S) "suffered from artifacts due to the way the diff was represented" (§4).

### Adjusted coarse-graining (§6.1)

Cells whose averages sit near a threshold boundary flip between buckets because of small fluctuations. This draws spurious edges, and the compressor counts them as complexity. The fix has two parts:

1. Use seven thresholds instead of the original three, so the buckets are finer.
2. Let each cell move up or down by one threshold if that shrinks the file. An exact search over all adjustments is infeasible (there are 2^{n²} of them), so a heuristic is used: a cell "within one threshold value of the majority value in its row" is set to that majority value.

### Analytic bound for the non-interacting case (§9, Appendix)

On an n × n periodic grid where each particle moves to one of its 4 neighbors uniformly at random:

- Claim 1: the expected particle count at any site stays at most 1 for all times.
- Summing over an L × L block and applying a Chernoff bound shows that blocks rarely deviate from their expectation, provided L ≫ G√(3 ln(2n²)) = Θ(G√log n).
- So, with high probability, the coarse-grained image is fixed by n and t, and its Kolmogorov complexity is at most log₂(n) + log₂(t) + O(1): no high apparent complexity at that scale.

## Limitations

- **The observer is built into the measure.** Grain size, the number of thresholds and the majority heuristic are all hand-chosen. The authors say themselves that the coarse-graining relies on human perception and that they want a more principled metric (§7).
- **Upper bounds only.** Compressed size bounds Kolmogorov complexity from above. Showing that complexity really is high needs a theoretical lower bound, which the paper leaves open (§7).
- **Under-specified experiments.** The grain size used in the experiments is not stated, and the sizes behind Figs. 6–8 are not listed. The paper does not say how runs end or whether Fig. 2 is one run or an average. Only Fig. 5 is explicitly labelled a single simulation.
- **Scaling claims are fits, not proofs.** The linear and quadratic trends rest on r² of curve fits over an unlisted set of sizes ("appear to increase").
- **The heuristic is row-based.** Using a row majority rather than a 2D neighborhood is a design choice that the paper does not justify further. (Own analysis)
- **Internal wording is not fully consistent.** §5.1 thresholds into "three buckets", while §6.1 speaks of "seven [thresholds], in contrast to the three used in the original experiment". §6.2 describes the non-interacting curve as "flattened to a lower value" and also says the adjustment "removes all of this estimated complexity". (Own analysis)

## Relation to other systems

Every comparison here is with a measure or tool the paper itself names.

- **OSCR (Evans et al.):** an optimal two-part-code approach that separates structure from noise. The authors' implementation was noisy on automaton data, so they replaced it with direct coarse-graining plus a generic compressor. Coarse-graining is cruder but more stable in their hands.
- **Light-cone complexity (Shalizi et al.):** defined through past/future mutual information, so it targets causal structure rather than spatial pattern. The paper judges it hard to compute and ill-suited to slowly changing systems, and lists it as future work.
- **Sophistication (Koppel; coarse sophistication):** the formal ideal that apparent complexity approximates under resource bounds. It is uncomputable and rarely large for short probabilistic programs.
- **Logical depth (Bennett):** measures computation time rather than description size. The paper rejects it because fast, simple programs can produce complex patterns.
- **gzip and bzip as estimators:** treated as practical stand-ins for K(x). The paper reports qualitatively similar complexity curves across compressors (Fig. 5).

## Takeaways for practice

All points in this section are (Own analysis), aimed at people building GPU training and inference systems.

- **Compressed size is a cheap, model-free signal of structure.** Compressing a coarse-grained snapshot, such as a bucketed GPU-fleet utilization heatmap, separates "uniform", "structured" and "noise" regimes without a hand-built model.
- **Coarse-grain before you measure.** Fine-grained compressed size tracked entropy, not structure. Aggregate telemetry (per node, per rack, per step) before looking for patterns, and report the scale.
- **Bucket boundaries create phantom signal.** Values near a threshold flip buckets and read as "complexity" (§6). Alert thresholds and histogram bins on noisy step-time or queue-depth metrics behave the same way. Use finer levels plus hysteresis or smoothing, as the paper's adjustment does.
- **Validate against a null model.** The provably simple non-interacting automaton exposed the metric's artifact. Give a straggler or anomaly detector a synthetic baseline where nothing happens, and check that it stays quiet.
- **Upper bounds need care.** A compressor shows a trace is at most this complex, never at least, so a high compressed size does not prove real structure.
- **Report every knob.** Missing grain sizes and run counts limit reproducibility here. Throughput benchmarks need the same: sizes, seeds, runs, aggregation.

# Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate

> **Why it matters:** It replaces the single fixed-length sentence vector of RNN encoder–decoders with a learned, differentiable soft search over per-token source annotations, the additive attention mechanism whose per-step cost and state shape later sequence-model systems inherit.

Notes are based on arXiv v7 (19 May 2016), PDF and arXiv HTML.

## Problem

Encoder–decoder NMT models of the time (Cho et al., 2014a; Sutskever et al., 2014, as described in Sec. 2) encode the whole source sentence into one fixed-length vector `c` and decode from it. The paper argues this vector is a bottleneck: the Introduction states that "the performance of a basic encoder–decoder deteriorates rapidly as the length of an input sentence increases" (citing Cho et al., 2014b). The goal is a model that does not have to squash all source information into a single vector, and that learns alignment and translation jointly, end to end, with no external aligner.

## Core design

**Encoder: bidirectional RNN annotations (Sec. 3.2).**
- A forward RNN and a backward RNN read the source; for each source position `j` the annotation is the concatenation `h_j = [h→_j ; h←_j]`, so it summarizes both the preceding and following words around `x_j`.
- The output of the encoder is therefore a variable-length sequence of `T_x` vectors, not one vector.
- Recurrent units are the gated hidden unit of Cho et al. (2014a), which the paper describes as LSTM-like (App. A.1.1).

**Decoder with a per-step context vector (Sec. 3.1, Eqs. 4–6).**
- `p(y_i | y_1..y_{i-1}, x) = g(y_{i-1}, s_i, c_i)`, with decoder state `s_i = f(s_{i-1}, y_{i-1}, c_i)`.
- The context is a weighted sum over all annotations: `c_i = Σ_j α_ij h_j`.
- Weights are a softmax over source positions: `α_ij = exp(e_ij) / Σ_k exp(e_ik)`, with score `e_ij = a(s_{i-1}, h_j)`.
- The alignment is soft, not a latent variable. Gradients flow through it, so the alignment model trains jointly with the rest of the network (Sec. 3.1).
- Section 3.1 says this design "relieve[s] the encoder from the burden of having to encode all information in the source sentence" into a fixed-length vector.

**Alignment model: additive scoring (App. A.1.2).**
- `a(s_{i-1}, h_j) = v_a^T tanh(W_a s_{i-1} + U_a h_j)`, a single-hidden-layer MLP.
- The paper notes the model is evaluated `T_x × T_y` times per sentence pair. That is why it is kept small, and why `U_a h_j` (independent of `i`) is precomputed once per source sentence.

**Output layer (App. A.2).** A deep output (Pascanu et al., 2014) with a single maxout hidden layer feeds the softmax over the target vocabulary.

**Training and decoding (Sec. 4.2, App. B).**
- Minibatch SGD with Adadelta, minibatch of 80 sentences, gradient-norm clipping.
- Batching: every 20th update the trainer pulls 1600 sentence pairs, sorts them by length and splits them into 20 minibatches. The data is shuffled once before training and traversed sequentially.
- Decoding uses beam search to approximately maximize the conditional probability.

## Limitations

- **Quadratic pairwise scoring.** Section 6.1 states that the method must compute an annotation weight for every source word for each target word. The paper calls this "not severe" for translation, given typical lengths of 15–40 words, but says it "may limit the applicability" to other tasks.
- **Rare and unknown words.** The 30k shortlist maps everything else to [UNK]. Under "All", every neural model in Table 1 remains below Moses. The Conclusion names better handling of unknown or rare words as an open challenge.
- **Sequential recurrence.** Both the encoder and the decoder are RNNs, so computation is serial over time steps. Attention adds work per decoder step instead of removing the recurrence. (Own analysis.)
- **Single language pair and short-ish inputs.** The experiments cover only En→Fr, with training on sentences of up to 30 or 50 words (RNN*-30/-50).
- **Unfair-baseline caveats.** Moses has an additional 418M-word monolingual corpus, while the neural models use only the parallel data (Sec. 4.1, 5.1). The model that edges past Moses on No UNK (RNNsearch-50*) also got a longer training budget than the others.

## Relation to other systems

- **RNN Encoder–Decoder (Cho et al., 2014a), the RNNencdec baseline.** It has the same gated units and decoder family but a single context vector `c`. RNNsearch differs only in the per-step `c_i` from attention plus the bidirectional encoder, and Table 1 shows it ahead at both training lengths.
- **Sequence to sequence with LSTMs (Sutskever et al., 2014).** Section 2 describes this model as using an LSTM encoder with `c = h_T`, the last hidden state. RNNsearch keeps all `T_x` states and reads them selectively at every step.
- **Graves (2013), handwriting synthesis.** That earlier alignment mechanism uses a mixture of Gaussian kernels whose modes "only move in one direction" (monotonic). RNNsearch scores every source position at every step, which allows reordering such as French adjective–noun inversion (Sec. 5.2.1, 6.1).
- **Neural features inside phrase-based SMT (Schwenk, 2012; Devlin et al., 2014; Cho et al., 2014a used for phrase scoring).** There, neural networks rescore or add a feature to Moses-style systems. RNNsearch is a standalone translator (Sec. 6.2).
- **Kalchbrenner and Blunsom (2013), recurrent continuous translation models.** This is earlier end-to-end neural translation, cited in Sec. 6.2. Unlike RNNsearch, it does not learn a soft alignment over per-token annotations. (Own analysis.)
- **Attention Is All You Need (NeurIPS 2017; see [its deep-read note](../google-transformer/1706.03762-attention-is-all-you-need-deep-read.md)).** That paper keeps the soft weighted sum but uses scaled dot-product scoring and drops recurrence. (Own analysis) The `T_x × T_y` scoring cost flagged in Sec. 6.1 then becomes a central systems cost, together with self-attention.

## Takeaways for practice

- **Attention cost scales with source length per output token.** Every decoder step scores all `T_x` source positions, so a full sequence costs on the order of `T_x × T_y` score evaluations (App. A.1.2). Budget compute and latency against the product of lengths, not just the output length. The paper's "not severe" verdict was tied to 15–40-word sentences. (Own analysis, building on Sec. 6.1.)
- **Hoist work that does not depend on the decoder step.** The paper precomputes `U_a h_j` once per source sentence. Engineers should do the same: project encoder outputs into key space once and reuse them across all decoding steps and beam hypotheses. (Own analysis, building on App. A.1.2.)
- **Decoding state grows with the input.** An encoder–decoder needs only `s_i` and one `c`. RNNsearch must keep all `T_x` annotations and their projections resident for the whole decode, for every sequence in the batch. Beam search can share them across hypotheses of the same source. This is the ancestor of the per-request KV-cache footprint. (Own analysis.)
- **Variable-length batching needs masking and length bucketing.** The paper sorts 1600 pairs by length into 20 minibatches to limit padding (App. B). With attention, padded source positions must also be masked out of the softmax, or they absorb probability mass. (Own analysis on masking; the bucketing scheme is from App. B.)
- **The softmax over source positions is a reduction per step.** Implementations should fuse the score, softmax and weighted sum to avoid materializing a `T_y × T_x` matrix when only the context vectors are needed. Keep the matrix only if you need alignments for debugging or visualization (Fig. 3). (Own analysis.)
- **Separate algorithmic gains from budget gains.** RNNsearch-50* versus RNNsearch-50 differs only in training length. When you compare systems, report results at matched training budgets. (Own analysis.)

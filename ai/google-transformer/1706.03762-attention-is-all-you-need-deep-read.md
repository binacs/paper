# Attention Is All You Need

> **Why it matters:** It replaces recurrence and convolution with self-attention, which needs a constant number of sequential operations per layer, and that choice sets the compute, memory and parallelism shape of the model (Own analysis: the shape later training and inference systems are built around).

Version used: arXiv v7 (2 Aug 2023), HTML and PDF. The paper's page-1 line reads "NIPS 2017, Long Beach".

## Problem

Recurrent encoder-decoders compute hidden state one position at a time, which prevents parallelism within a training example (Sec. 1). Convolutional models (ByteNet, ConvS2S) parallelize across positions, but the operations needed to relate two distant positions grow with distance (Sec. 2). The paper wants layers that relate any two positions in a constant number of operations, and that train faster without losing translation quality.

## Core design

**Encoder-decoder stacks (Sec. 3.1).**
- Encoder: N = 6 identical layers, each with a multi-head self-attention sub-layer and a position-wise feed-forward sub-layer. Each sub-layer is wrapped as `LayerNorm(x + Sublayer(x))` (post-norm residual). Every sub-layer and the embeddings output vectors of width d_model = 512.
- Decoder: N = 6 layers with a third sub-layer that attends over the encoder output. Decoder self-attention is masked, and output embeddings are offset by one position, so the prediction at position i depends only on outputs before i.

**Scaled dot-product attention (Sec. 3.2.1).** `Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q K^T / sqrt(d_k)) V`, computed as matrix products over all queries at once. The scale counters dot-product variance growing with d_k (footnote 4), which would otherwise push softmax into regions with tiny gradients. Dot-product was chosen over additive attention for speed and space efficiency via optimized matmul.

**Multi-head attention (Sec. 3.2.2).** Q, K and V are projected h = 8 times into d_k = d_v = 64. The heads run in parallel, and their outputs are concatenated and projected. Total compute is similar to one full-width head.

**Three uses of attention (Sec. 3.2.3).**
- Encoder-decoder attention: queries come from the decoder, keys and values from the encoder output.
- Encoder self-attention: every position attends to all positions.
- Decoder self-attention: causal, with illegal positions set to −∞ before the softmax.

**Other parts (Sec. 3.3–3.5).**
- Feed-forward: a position-wise ReLU MLP with d_ff = 2048.
- Weight tying: one matrix is shared by both embeddings and the pre-softmax projection.
- Positions: sinusoidal encodings are added to the embeddings. Learned positional embeddings performed nearly identically (Table 3 row E).

**Why self-attention (Sec. 4, Table 1).** With n the sequence length and d the width, self-attention costs O(n²·d) per layer with O(1) sequential operations and O(1) maximum path length. Recurrence costs O(n·d²) with O(n) sequential operations and O(n) path length. Convolution costs O(k·n·d²) with O(log_k n) path length. Restricted attention over r neighbours costs O(r·n·d) with O(n/r) path length. Self-attention is cheaper than recurrence when n < d, which the paper notes is typical for sentence-level translation.

**Training and inference recipe (Sec. 5, 6.1).**
- Training: batches are built by approximate length, at about 25,000 source and 25,000 target tokens each. Training ran on one machine with 8 NVIDIA P100 GPUs.
- Optimizer: Adam, with a learning rate that rises linearly for 4,000 warmup steps and then decays with the inverse square root of the step number.
- Regularization: residual dropout (P_drop = 0.1 for base) and label smoothing ε_ls = 0.1, which hurts perplexity but helps BLEU.
- Decoding: beam size 4, α = 0.6, maximum output length of input + 50.
- Checkpoint averaging: checkpoints are written at 10-minute intervals, and the last 5 (base) or 20 (big) are averaged.

## Limitations

- **Quadratic attention.** Self-attention is O(n²·d) per layer (Table 1), and the efficiency argument assumes n < d, as for sentences. Restricted attention for long inputs is left to future work (Sec. 4, 7).
- **Sequential decoding.** Generation stays autoregressive. Making it less sequential is listed as future work (Sec. 7).
- **Narrow evaluation.** The paper covers two WMT14 language pairs and parsing on one 8-GPU machine. It has no multi-node runs, no long sequences and no measured inference latency.
- **Estimated cost.** Training FLOPs are estimates from assumed sustained TFLOPS. Sinusoidal length extrapolation is stated only as a hypothesis (Sec. 3.5).
- **Inconsistencies inside the paper (v7).**
  - EN-FR big BLEU is 41.0 in the Sec. 6.1 text but 41.8 in the abstract and Table 2 (the v5 abstract also says 41.8).
  - Sec. 6.3 says the parser beats all previously reported models except the RNN Grammar. Table 4 also lists Luong et al. (2015) multi-task at 93.0, above the Transformer's 92.7, though in a different training-setting group.
  - Table 2 gives one training-cost value per Transformer row under a two-column (EN-DE/EN-FR) header, so it is unclear whether the value covers both language pairs.
- (Own analysis) The paper does not treat the O(n²) score matrix or the growing decoder state as memory problems.

## Relation to other systems

- **GNMT + RL.** An LSTM encoder-decoder with O(n) sequential operations per layer. It reaches 24.6 EN-DE BLEU at 2.3·10¹⁹ FLOPs, the same estimated cost as Transformer big (Table 2).
- **ConvS2S.** Convolutional and parallel across positions, but the path between distant positions grows linearly (Sec. 2). It reaches 25.16 EN-DE BLEU at 9.6·10¹⁸ FLOPs (Table 2).
- **ByteNet.** Dilated convolutions give a logarithmic path length (Sec. 2) and 23.75 EN-DE BLEU (Table 2). The Transformer makes the path length constant.
- **Sparsely-gated MoE.** Recurrent layers with conditional computation, at 26.03 EN-DE BLEU for 2.0·10¹⁹ FLOPs (Table 2). (Own analysis) The Transformer gets its efficiency from dense parallel matmuls rather than from sparsity. The two ideas are orthogonal.
- **RNN Grammar.** The parsing baseline the paper says it does not beat (Sec. 6.3, Table 4).
## Takeaways for practice

- **Compute shape.** Attention is O(n²·d) per layer, and the feed-forward is position-wise (Table 1, Sec. 3.3). (Own analysis) At short n, the projection and feed-forward GEMMs dominate. At long n, the score/softmax term dominates. Pick kernels and sequence-length caps for your regime.
- **Memory shape.** (Own analysis) A naive implementation stores an n×n score matrix per head per layer, so activation memory grows quadratically with length. Fused or tiled kernels and recomputation exist to avoid this.
- **Parallelism.** O(1) sequential operations per layer let training parallelize across positions (Table 1). (Own analysis) Heads are independent until the output projection (Sec. 3.2.2), which suits head-wise tensor parallelism.
- **KV state.** Decoding is autoregressive under a causal mask (Sec. 3.2.3). (Own analysis) Keys and values of earlier positions do not change, so they can be cached. Cross-attention K/V are computed once from the encoder. The cache grows with layers × length × d_model per sequence, so KV memory, not FLOPs, caps serving batch size and context.
- **Batch by tokens.** The paper batches by approximate length, at about 25K source + 25K target tokens (Sec. 5.1). (Own analysis) Token budgets keep memory and step time stable for schedulers that pack jobs by memory.
- **Heads are a hardware knob.** In Table 3 (A), heads are varied at constant compute; Sec. 6.2 says single-head attention is worse than the best setting and that quality also drops with too many heads. (Own analysis) Between those extremes, head dimension can be chosen for kernel and tensor-core fit.
- **Checkpointing.** Averaging checkpoints taken every 10 minutes (Sec. 6.1) improves results at no extra training cost. Weight tying across embeddings and softmax (Sec. 3.4) cuts parameters. (Own analysis) Tying couples the first and last layers when a model is split into pipeline stages.
- **FLOPs claims.** Table 2 costs are estimates from time × GPUs × assumed TFLOPS. Measured utilization is the better basis for comparing systems.

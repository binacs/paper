# Variational Lossy Autoencoder

> **Why it matters:** It explains why a VAE with a powerful autoregressive decoder learns to ignore its latent code, and turns that failure into a control knob. By limiting what the decoder can see, you choose which information must flow through the latent and which stays in the decoder.

Version used: arXiv v2 (first-page stamp "arXiv:1611.02731v2 [cs.LG] 4 Mar 2017", header "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2017"). The arXiv page lists no official code repository.

## Problem

Representation learning wants a latent code that captures global factors such as object shape, not local texture. Two model families pull in different directions (Sec. 1):

- **Autoregressive models** (PixelRNN/PixelCNN) are strong density estimators but have no stochastic latents.
- **VAEs** learn a code, but earlier hybrids with expressive autoregressive decoders often left it unused. This was usually blamed on optimization and patched with KL annealing or decoder dropout.

The paper argues that the code is ignored even at the optimum, and asks how to make an expressive VAE keep a *useful, lossy* code on purpose.

## Core design

### Why the latent is ignored: a bits-back argument (Sec. 2.2)
- The paper treats variational inference as bits-back coding. The expected code length for x is at least the data entropy plus a penalty equal to the KL gap between the approximate posterior q(z|x) and the true posterior p(z|x).
- Suppose the decoder p(x|z) can model the data well without z. Then the cheapest solution sets the true posterior to the prior and q(z|x) to the prior, so z carries nothing and the inefficiency penalty is zero. This holds even with perfect optimization.
- The resulting **"information preference"** property: information that the decoder can model locally, without z, gets encoded by the decoder. Only the rest goes into z.

### Lossy code by explicit information placement (Sec. 3.1)
- Use the property deliberately. The decoder stays autoregressive but conditions only on a **small local window** of previously generated pixels, not the full history.
- Local statistics such as texture can then be modeled from the window alone and stay out of z. Long-range global structure cannot be predicted from the window and must pass through z.
- The decoder's receptive field therefore defines what the code *loses*. The receptive field is a design choice that has to match the downstream task.

### Learned prior via autoregressive flow (Sec. 3.2)
- The prior p(z) is parameterized as an **autoregressive flow (AF)** applied to simple noise, not a fixed factorized Gaussian. A more expressive prior can narrow the gap between the approximate and true posterior.
- **AF prior vs IAF posterior:** rewriting the bound shows that an AF prior is equivalent to an inverse autoregressive flow (IAF) posterior on the encoder path. The difference is that the AF prior gives a deeper decoder path. The paper says both cost the same in expectation, so the AF prior adds expressiveness at no extra training cost.

### Concrete architectures (Appendix A, B)
- **Binary images:** a ResNet VAE (from the IAF MNIST setup) whose output map is concatenated with the image and fed to a small masked PixelCNN (6 masked conv layers plus 4 1x1 ResNet blocks). The latent has dimension 64. The prior uses 4 mean-only AF steps, each a 3-layer MADE with 640 hidden units. Training uses Adamax (lr 0.002), "free bits" and Polyak averaging (α = 0.998).
- **CIFAR10:** the latent is 16 feature maps of 8x8. The prior is a factorized Gaussian transformed by 6 AF steps, each a PixelCNN with 2 hidden layers and 128 feature maps. The decoder is a conditional, channel-autoregressive PixelCNN++ with 4 blocks of 64 feature maps. The receptive fields tried are 4x2, 5x3 and 7x4, plus a 7x4 variant whose window sees only grayscale.
- Hyperparameters were tuned on statically binarized MNIST and reused on the other datasets (Sec. 4.2).

## Limitations

- **What is "lost" is hand-designed.** The receptive field fixes the split between local and global information. On OMNIGLOT some semantics are not preserved in reconstructions, and the paper says the lossy statistics must be chosen per task (Sec. 4.1).
- **The asymptotic argument needs optimization help.** Information preference is described as an asymptotic view that holds only when the bound is well optimized. In practice the paper still uses free bits and mentions KL annealing.
- **Slow sampling.** The paper states that VLAE "is slower at generation due to the sequential nature of autoregressive model" (Conclusion).
- **Limited tuning.** Hyperparameters were tuned on static MNIST only. CIFAR10 likelihoods are importance-sampled estimates, not exact values.
- **Scope.** Images only. Audio, video and semi-supervised use of task-specific codes are left as future work.
- (Own analysis) In Appendix D the AF prior's NLL gain over a Gaussian prior is small next to the decoder's; the prior mostly raises how much the code carries (KL 11.73 vs 10.60).

## Relation to other systems

All comparisons are to work the paper itself names.

- **PixelRNN / PixelCNN / PixelCNN++ (van den Oord et al.; Salimans et al.):** pure autoregressive models with no latent code. VLAE reuses PixelCNN as a *window-limited* decoder. On CIFAR10 PixelCNN++ is slightly better in bits/dim (Table 5), but VLAE also yields a code.
- **IAF VAE (Kingma et al., 2016):** an expressive posterior with a factorized decoder. VLAE moves the flow to the prior (shown equivalent to an IAF posterior with a deeper decoder path) and adds an autoregressive decoder. It improves on IAF VAE on both MNIST variants and on CIFAR10.
- **PixelVAE (Gulrajani et al., 2016):** concurrent work, also a conditional PixelCNN decoder, with several levels of stochastic units. VLAE adds the bits-back explanation, the receptive-field restriction and the AF prior.
- **Bowman et al. (2015):** weakened an over-expressive text decoder with dropout, plus KL annealing. VLAE instead restricts the decoder's view structurally, choosing *which* information it can model, not just how well.
- **LVAE (Kaae Sønderby et al.) and related deep autoregressive priors:** the paper says their autoregressive likelihood is intractable and has to be inferred variationally. VLAE's AF prior has exact likelihood across multiple flow steps.
- **Convolutional DRAW (Gregor et al., 2016):** its high-level latents also give a lossy compression. VLAE uses the autoregressive decoder to control what is lost, and reports better CIFAR10 bits/dim (Table 5).

## Takeaways for practice

For someone building GPU training and inference systems. All points are (Own analysis).

- **Watch KL as a training-health metric.** A latent that collapses to the prior is an optimum the objective prefers, not just a bug, when the decoder is strong. Log per-step KL (here 13.3 nats vs 37.3 bits for a factorized decoder) next to loss, and alert when it drops to zero.
- **Architecture choices set information flow.** Receptive field, masking and conditioning windows decide what the conditioning signal has to carry. Treat them as model hyperparameters to sweep, not kernel details, and keep masked-conv kernels configurable by window shape (4x2, 5x3, 7x4 here).
- **Sampling cost is the bill for autoregressive decoders.** Generation is sequential per pixel, so serving needs incremental decoding with cached activations and cross-request batching. A smaller receptive field means less state per step.
- **Eval needs importance sampling.** Reported likelihoods use 512 importance samples per example. Budget eval jobs for that and record the sample count with every number.
- **Optimization aids are load-bearing.** Free bits and Polyak averaging belong in versioned config, not ad-hoc patches.

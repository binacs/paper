# Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition

> **Why it matters:** Identity shortcuts make depth an optimization-cheap knob. The paper trains 152-layer ImageNet models that need fewer FLOPs than VGG-16/19 and are more accurate, so from here on depth is set by a compute budget rather than by whether the net trains at all.

Read from arXiv 1512.03385v1 (the only version listed, "Tech report", 10 Dec 2015). Section and table numbers below refer to that version.

## Problem

Plain stacked convolutional nets get worse as they get deeper, even on the training set. In Sec. 4.1 the 34-layer plain net "has higher training error throughout the whole training procedure" than the 18-layer one. The authors call this *degradation* and argue it is an optimization problem, not overfitting. They also argue it is not vanishing gradients: the plain nets use BN, which keeps forward signals at non-zero variance, and they report that backward gradient norms are healthy. Their conjecture is that deep plain nets "may have exponentially low convergence rates", and they leave the real cause to future work.

The constructive argument (Sec. 1): a deeper net could copy a shallower one and add identity layers, so it should never be worse. Solvers evidently fail to find that solution in practice.

## Core design

### Residual reformulation (Sec. 3.1–3.2)
- Each block learns F(x) := H(x) − x and outputs y = F(x, {W_i}) + x. If identity is close to optimal, the solver only has to push F toward zero.
- The identity shortcut adds **no parameters and no extra computation** apart from an element-wise add. So plain and residual nets of the same depth have the same cost, which makes the comparison clean.
- When dimensions change, the shortcut is either a projection y = F(x) + W_s x or zero-padded identity.

### Shortcut options (Sec. 4.1, Table 3)
- **A:** zero-padding where dimensions increase; every shortcut is parameter-free.
- **B:** projections only where dimensions increase; identity elsewhere.
- **C:** every shortcut is a projection (thirteen extra projection shortcuts in ResNet-34).
- The paper finds B slightly better than A and C marginally better than B. It concludes that projections are "not essential" for fixing degradation and uses B for the deeper nets.

### Architectures (Sec. 3.3, Table 1)
- The plain baseline follows VGG: mostly 3×3 convolutions, filters doubling when the map is halved, stride-2 downsampling, and global average pooling into a 1000-way FC layer. Four stages (conv2_x–conv5_x) run at 56², 28², 14² and 7².
- 18/34-layer nets use **basic blocks**: two 3×3 convolutions.
- 50/101/152-layer nets use **bottleneck blocks**: 1×1 reduce, 3×3, then 1×1 restore, with inner widths 64–512 and output widths 256–2048. Identity shortcuts matter most here: the paper says a projection shortcut in a bottleneck would double time complexity and model size, because it connects the two high-dimensional ends.
- Per-stage block counts are [3,4,6,3] for ResNet-50, [3,4,23,3] for ResNet-101 and [3,8,36,3] for ResNet-152 (Table 1).

### Training recipe (Sec. 3.4)
- Scale augmentation samples the shorter side from [256, 480]. A 224×224 crop is taken from the image or its flip, the per-pixel mean is subtracted, and colour augmentation is added.
- BN sits right after each convolution and before the activation. Weights are initialized as in PReLU-net [13]. No dropout.
- SGD with mini-batch 256, LR 0.1 divided by 10 when error plateaus, up to 60×10⁴ iterations, weight decay 0.0001, momentum 0.9.
- Test-time evaluation uses 10-crop for the ablations. The best results use a fully-convolutional net with scores averaged over shorter sides {224, 256, 384, 480, 640}.

### CIFAR-10 study (Sec. 4.2)
- The CIFAR nets have 6n+2 weighted layers on maps of {32, 16, 8} with {16, 32, 64} filters and identity (option A) shortcuts everywhere.
- Training uses batch 128 on two GPUs. LR 0.1 is divided by 10 at 32k and 48k iterations, and training stops at 64k. The schedule was chosen on a 45k/5k split.
- The 110-layer net warms up at LR 0.01 until training error falls below 80% (about 400 iterations).

### Detection transfer (Sec. 4.3, Appendix A)
- ResNet-101 replaces VGG-16 inside an otherwise unchanged Faster R-CNN; layers up to conv4_x are shared, RoI pooling sits before conv5_1, and conv5_x is the per-region head.

## Limitations

- **No systems metrics.** The paper reports no wall-clock training time, no throughput, no activation memory and no ImageNet GPU count. FLOPs are the only cost figure given.
- **Theory left open.** The cause of degradation is a conjecture ("exponentially low convergence rates"), and the paper offers no analysis of why residual learning fixes it beyond the preconditioning intuition (Sec. 2, 3.1).
- **Depth stops paying at some point.** The 1202-layer CIFAR net trains to <0.1% error but tests worse than the 110-layer net. The authors put this down to overfitting and note they used no maxout or dropout.
- **The schedule is not fully specified.** For ImageNet, LR drops happen "when the error plateaus", which is not a fixed recipe. Only ResNet-110 on CIFAR is reported over several runs; the ImageNet ablations are single numbers.
- **Mixed comparison.** The single-model 4.49% (val) is said to beat all previous ensembles (Table 5, test). Those are different evaluation sets.
- (Own analysis) Equal FLOPs do not mean equal latency or memory. The bottleneck's 1×1 layers and the long chains of sequential layers change arithmetic intensity and activation footprint compared with VGG, and the paper measures neither.

## Relation to other systems

- **VGG [41]:** The plain baseline copies VGG's design rules. ResNet-34 has 3.6×10⁹ FLOPs, which the paper calls 18% of VGG-19, and even ResNet-152 is below VGG-16/19 in FLOPs. (Own analysis) The shortcuts add no cost, so this gap comes from the baseline's design (stride-2 downsampling, global pooling instead of large FC layers, bottlenecks), not from residual learning itself. The paper gives no per-layer breakdown of the cost.
- **GoogLeNet / Inception [44]:** The paper describes an inception layer as "a shortcut branch and a few deeper branches", plus auxiliary classifiers against gradient problems. ResNet drops the auxiliary heads and multi-branch design in favour of one uniform additive identity path.
- **Highway Networks [42, 43]:** These have gated shortcuts that depend on the data and carry parameters, and a gate can close and block the shortcut. ResNet's shortcuts are parameter-free and never closed. Per the paper, highway nets had not shown accuracy gains beyond about 100 layers. On CIFAR-10 (Table 6), Highway-19 with 2.3M params reaches 7.54%.
- **BN-Inception [16]:** ResNet uses BN in every block (conv → BN → ReLU), but BN alone does not prevent degradation in plain nets (Sec. 4.1).
- **PReLU-net [13]:** ResNet takes its weight initialization from PReLU-net and beats it in Tables 3–4.
- **Faster R-CNN [32]:** The detector stays fixed while the backbone is swapped, so the detection gains are attributed to the representation alone.

## Takeaways for practice

- **Depth and compute are separate knobs.** ResNet-34 and ResNet-50 cost 3.6 and 3.8 ×10⁹ multiply-adds (Table 1), because bottlenecks move channel width into cheap 1×1 convolutions. When sizing a cluster job, read the per-model FLOP figure, not the layer count.
- **Per-model FLOPs are given in multiply-adds.** (Own analysis) Treat the Table 1 figures as MACs for a single 224×224 forward pass. Converting to hardware FLOP counts or training cost per step needs the usual MAC-to-FLOP and forward-to-backward factors, which the paper does not state.
- **Activation memory grows with depth even when FLOPs don't.** (Own analysis) Every block keeps its input for the residual add and BN statistics for backward. A 152-layer net therefore holds many more stored activations than its FLOP count suggests. Plan for activation checkpointing or smaller per-GPU batches when going deep.
- **The identity path has to stay cheap.** The paper's own warning is that projection shortcuts in bottlenecks double time and model size. (Own analysis) In fused-kernel or compiler pipelines, keep the residual add as a light element-wise op and do not materialize extra copies.
- **The recipe is short and reusable.** It is SGD at batch 256, LR 0.1 with ÷10 steps, momentum 0.9, weight decay 1e-4, BN after every conv, no dropout, and up to 60×10⁴ iterations. Very deep nets may need a low-LR warm-up, as the 110-layer CIFAR net did (0.01 for about 400 iterations).
- **Use the degradation test as a health check.** Compare *training* error of a shallow and a deep variant; if the deeper one trains worse, the problem is optimization, and more regularization will not help.
- **Headline accuracy has an evaluation cost.** The 3.57% is an ensemble, and the best single-model numbers average five test scales. Budget evaluation compute for the protocol being reproduced, not for one forward pass.

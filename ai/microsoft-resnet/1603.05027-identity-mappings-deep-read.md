# Identity Mappings in Deep Residual Networks

> **Why it matters:** It shows that a residual stack trains reliably at 1000+ layers only if the skip path stays a clean identity, both on the shortcut and after the addition, and it introduces the "pre-activation" unit (BN and ReLU before each weight layer) that makes this hold.

Notes are based on arXiv v3 (2016-07-25), read from the arXiv PDF.

## Problem

The original ResNet unit computes `y_l = h(x_l) + F(x_l, W_l)` and `x_{l+1} = f(y_l)`, with `h` an identity shortcut and `f` a ReLU applied after the addition (Sec. 2, Eqns. 1–2). That design trained well at about 100 layers. Pushed to about 1000 layers, it got worse: with the original unit, ResNet-1001 has higher CIFAR-10 error than ResNet-164 (Table 3), and "the training error is reduced very slowly at the beginning of training" (Sec. 4.2). The paper asks which parts of the unit stop the signal from flowing through very deep stacks. It also asks whether alternatives such as scaling, gating, 1×1 projections or dropout on the shortcut help or hurt.

## Core design

### Analysis: make both `h` and `f` identity

- If `f` is also identity, `x_{l+1} = x_l + F(x_l, W_l)` (Eqn. 3). Unrolled, this gives `x_L = x_l + Σ_{i=l}^{L-1} F(x_i, W_i)` (Eqn. 4). Any deeper feature is a shallower feature plus a sum of residuals, not a product of layer transforms.
- Backward pass (Eqn. 5): `∂E/∂x_l = ∂E/∂x_L · (1 + ∂/∂x_l Σ F(x_i, W_i))`. The "1" term carries the gradient straight from any deeper unit to any shallower unit "without concerning any weight layers", so the gradient is less likely to vanish.
- Counter-example (Sec. 3, Eqns. 6–7): with a scaled shortcut `h(x_l) = λ_l x_l`, the direct term picks up a factor `Π λ_i`. For large depth, this factor "can be exponentially large" if every λ_i > 1. If every λ_i < 1, it "can be exponentially small and vanish, which blocks the backpropagated signal from the shortcut". Gates and 1×1 convs act like multiplicative modulations on this path too.

### Shortcut ablations (Sec. 3.1, Table 1, ResNet-110 on CIFAR-10)

- Variants tested: constant scaling, exclusive gating (Highway-style `1−g(x)` on the shortcut and `g(x)` on F, with `g(x) = σ(W_g x + b_g)`), shortcut-only gating, 1×1 conv shortcut, and dropout (ratio 0.5) on the shortcut.
- None beats the identity. Several "fail" (the paper defines this as test error above 20%). Shortcut-only gating gets close (6.91) only with bias init b_g = −6. (Own analysis) A strongly negative b_g keeps `1−g(x)` near 1, so the shortcut stays close to identity.
- Sec. 3.2: these variants can represent an identity, so they are at least as expressive. Their higher training error therefore points to "optimization issues", not representational limits. A 1×1 conv shortcut had helped a 34-layer ResNet with 16 units in the original paper (option C), "but we find that this is not the case when there are many Residual Units."

### Activation placement and the pre-activation unit (Sec. 4, Table 2)

- Variants tested: BN after the addition; ReLU before the addition, which makes the residual output non-negative and so limits its range; ReLU-only pre-activation; and full pre-activation.
- Key reframing (Sec. 4.1): make the after-addition activation asymmetric, so that it affects only the F path of the next unit. It then becomes that unit's *pre-activation*. The full pre-activation unit is BN → ReLU → weight → BN → ReLU → weight, plus an untouched identity shortcut.
- Boundary handling (Appendix): the first activation sits right after conv1, before the split into two paths. One extra activation follows the last element-wise addition.
- Two effects claimed (Sec. 4.2):
  - **Easier optimization.** With `f = ReLU`, a negative signal on the trunk is truncated. This is "not severe" at 164 layers but "more frequent when there are 1000 layers".
  - **Less overfitting.** In the original unit, BN output is "soon added to the shortcut", so the merged signal is not normalized. In the pre-activation unit, "the inputs to all weight layers have been normalized". The paper attributes the better test error "presumably" to BN's regularization.
- Deep CIFAR models use the bottleneck unit from the original ResNet paper (1×1 reduce, 3×3, 1×1 restore). Its "computational complexity is similar to the two-3×3 Residual Unit" (Sec. 4.1).

### Training recipe (Appendix)

- **CIFAR:** LR 0.1, divided by 10 at 32k and 48k iterations. Mini-batch 128 on 2 GPUs (64 each). Weight decay 0.0001, momentum 0.9. Translation and flip augmentation only.
- **CIFAR warm-up:** LR 0.01 for the first 400 iterations, then back to 0.1. The paper remarks that this "is not necessary for our proposed Residual Unit."
- **ImageNet:** LR 0.1 with no warm-up, divided by 10 at 30 and 60 epochs. Mini-batch 256 on 8 GPUs (32 each). Same augmentation as the original ResNet paper.

## Limitations

- **Evidence is mostly CIFAR-scale.** The gating, scaling and 1×1 shortcut variants were tried on ImageNet only in preliminary ResNet-101 runs. The authors "decided to halt training due to limited resources" (Sec. 5).
- **The analysis is idealized.** Eqns. 4–5 assume a pure identity path. Real nets still have projection or downsampling shortcuts between stages. The paper treats these as few and does not analyze them.
- **The overfitting explanation is hedged.** The regularization benefit is "presumably" from BN (Sec. 4.2) and is not isolated by a dedicated experiment.
- **Some comparisons are not like-for-like.** The ResNet-152 ImageNet gain is small (Table 5). The headline 4.62 on CIFAR-10 used a mini-batch of 64, while every other ResNet result in Table 4, including the 4.92 one, used 128. The paper does not discuss why the smaller batch helped.
- **No systems cost reporting.** The paper gives wall-clock time only. It says nothing about memory, throughput or per-GPU utilization at 1000 layers.
- **BN-dependent design** (Own analysis). Behaviour with very small per-device batches is not studied.

## Relation to other systems

- **Original ResNet (He et al., arXiv 1512.03385).** It uses the same shortcut and a post-addition ReLU. Under the same hyper-parameters, this paper reports that the original unit degrades at 1001 layers on CIFAR and overfits at 200 layers on ImageNet: the original ResNet-200's error is "higher than the baseline ResNet-152" while its training error is lower (Sec. 5, Table 5). The original paper's option C (1×1 projection shortcuts) helped at 34 layers. It is shown to hurt with many units (Sec. 3.1).
- **Highway Networks / "Training very deep networks".** The exclusive-gating variant follows Highway gating (Sec. 3.1). Gating needs careful bias init and still trails the identity (Table 1). In Table 4, Highway reports 7.72 on CIFAR-10, against 4.92 for pre-act ResNet-1001.
- **Inception v3.** Table 5 lists 21.2 / 5.6 top-1 / top-5 with 299×299 crops. Pre-act ResNet-200 with scale and aspect-ratio augmentation reports 20.1 / 4.8.
- **FitNet + LSUV init, ELU, All-CNN.** These are the CIFAR baselines in Table 4. (Own analysis) They improve deep-net training through initialization or activation choice rather than through the topology of the skip path.
- **Batch Normalization.** BN is the normalization the pre-activation unit is built around. The paper's point is about *where* BN sits relative to the addition, not about replacing it.
- **Pre-normalization in later architectures** (Own analysis). Putting normalization inside the residual branch while keeping the trunk an untouched sum is the same structural idea that later deep Transformer stacks adopted ("pre-LN"). This paper does not discuss Transformers.

## Takeaways for practice

- **Keep the residual trunk a pure sum.** Every scale, gate, projection or dropout on the shortcut puts a factor into a depth-long product (Eqns. 6–7). If a stability or regularization trick is needed, put it inside F, not on the skip path.
- **Put normalization and nonlinearity inside the branch.** Do not put them after the addition. Post-addition BN and post-addition ReLU were both measurably worse (Table 2), and the gap grows with depth (Table 3). Do not forget the extra activation after the final addition (Appendix).
- **Read early-training loss curves as a health check** (Own analysis). The paper's symptom of a broken signal path at 1000 layers was a training error that dropped very slowly at the start (Sec. 4.2). Check a slow first phase against the architecture, not only the LR schedule.
- **Warm-up may hide a topology problem** (Own analysis). The paper warmed up all CIFAR runs but says the warm-up is unnecessary for the pre-activation unit (Appendix). If a deep model needs warm-up to train at all, check the skip path first.
- **Depth is cheap in parameters, not in wall-clock time.** ResNet-1001 has 10.2M parameters (Table 4) but took about 27 hours on 2 GPUs (Sec. 5). ResNet-200 took about 3 weeks on 8 GPUs. (Own analysis) Activation memory and sequential depth, not parameter count, are what scale. Plan activation checkpointing and pipeline partitioning around the number of units. With pre-activation, cut points along an identity trunk are natural boundaries.
- **Mind per-GPU BN batch size** (Own analysis). The recipes split mini-batches into 64 (CIFAR) or 32 (ImageNet) per GPU (Appendix). The 4.62 result used a total mini-batch of 64 (Table 4). Changing the device count changes the statistics BN regularizes with, so keep per-device batch fixed when you scale out, or account for it explicitly.
- **Prefer init-robust designs.** Gating only worked within a narrow b_g init window (Table 1). (Own analysis) Fragile init sensitivity means more failed runs at scale.

# Multi-Scale Context Aggregation by Dilated Convolutions

> **Why it matters:** The paper shows that stacking dilated 3×3 convolutions with growing dilation gives a receptive field that grows exponentially while parameters grow linearly. Resolution is never lost along the way, so a small, identity-initialized "context module" can be added to an existing dense-prediction network and improve accuracy.

Read from arXiv 1511.07122v3 (stamped "30 Apr 2016", with the line "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2016"). Section, table and appendix numbers refer to that version. The paper reports no runtime, memory or GPU figures.

## Problem

Semantic segmentation needs pixel-level accuracy and multi-scale context at once. Networks adapted from classification get their global view by pooling and subsampling, which discards resolution. Earlier fixes recover resolution with up-convolutions or run on several rescaled inputs, and the authors ask whether those costs are needed (Sec. 1). Two questions follow: can context be aggregated *without* pooling or rescaling, and which parts of a repurposed classification network actually hurt dense prediction?

## Core design

### Dilated convolution (Sec. 2)
- An *l-dilated convolution* samples the input at steps of l; l = 1 is ordinary convolution. No "dilated filter" is built. A footnote separates the operator from the *algorithme à trous*, a wavelet algorithm that uses it.
- If 3×3 filters are stacked with dilations 1, 2, 4, …, the receptive field of each element in F_{i+1} is (2^{i+2}−1)×(2^{i+2}−1). The field grows exponentially while parameters grow linearly, and no resolution or coverage is lost (Sec. 2, Fig. 1).

### Context module (Sec. 3, Table 1)
- The module is "a rectangular prism of convolutional layers, with no pooling or subsampling" (Sec. 1). C feature maps in, C out, so it fits on top of any front end that emits per-class score maps.
- **Basic module:** seven 3×3 layers with dilations 1, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 1, each followed by the truncation max(·,0), then a final 1×1 layer. Every layer has C channels.
- The exponential growth stops after layer 6 because the front end's output is only 64×64.
- **Large module:** the same dilations with wider layers (2C, 2C, 4C, 8C, 16C, 32C, 32C, then C).
- **Identity initialization:** filters start so that "each layer simply passes the input directly to the next" (Eq. 4), following identity initialization for recurrent networks (Le et al., 2015). "Random initialization schemes were not effective for the context module." For the Large module, Eq. 5 generalizes this and adds Gaussian noise with σ ≪ C/c_{i+1} (no numeric value).

### Front end: simplifying VGG-16 (Sec. 4)
- The authors start from VGG-16 and "remove the last two pooling and striding layers entirely".
- Later convolutions are dilated by 2 per removed pooling layer, so the final layers are dilated by 4, keeping pretrained weights usable at higher resolution.
- Intermediate padding is removed ("neither necessary nor justified in dense prediction"); the input gets reflection padding. Output is 64×64 maps with no up-convolution or multi-scale input.

### Training (Sec. 4–5, App. A)
- VOC-2012: front end first, then the context module on its output; joint training "did not yield a significant improvement" (Sec. 5). The front end trains on VOC-2012 plus COCO images containing VOC categories, then fine-tunes on VOC-2012 only.
- The module is evaluated alone, with a dense CRF, and with CRF-RNN.
- Appendix A re-sizes the module per dataset: Dilation8 (CamVid), Dilation7 (KITTI, layer 6 removed), Dilation10 (Cityscapes, two added layers with dilation 32 and 64), all without structured prediction.

## Limitations

- **No cost accounting:** no runtime, memory, throughput or hardware numbers; the case for dilation rests on parameters and receptive field.
- **Per-dataset re-tuning:** the module is sized to the 64×64 front-end output and layers are removed (KITTI) or added (Cityscapes), so it is not one fixed recipe.
- **Classification pre-training remains:** dense end-to-end training without it is left as future work (Sec. 6).
- **Initialization is load-bearing:** random initialization failed; the Large module's noise scale is only an inequality.
- **Mixed regimes:** no joint training on VOC-2012, a joint stage on Cityscapes, not reconciled.
- **CRFs still help:** the best VOC-2012 test result (75.3) adds CRF-RNN on top of the module; Fig. 4 shows failure cases.

## Relation to other systems

- **FCN-8s (Long et al., 2015):** recovers resolution after subsampling and takes skip connections from coarser layers. This paper keeps resolution by deleting the last two pooling/striding layers and dilating the convolutions that follow. Its front end beats FCN-8s by more than 5 points on VOC-2012 test (Table 2).
- **DeepLab / DeepLab-LargeFOV / DeepLab-MSc (Chen et al., 2015a):** also adapted from VGG-16, with dilation. The paper's front end removes further components (intermediate padding) and uses no multi-scale input. DeepLab-LFOV is the main baseline in Appendix A, where Dilation8 and Dilation7 beat it on CamVid and KITTI.
- **Dense CRF (Krähenbühl & Koltun, 2011) and CRF-RNN (Zheng et al., 2015):** structured-prediction stages after the network. The context module sits *before* them and helps in each configuration (Table 3).
- **Up-convolution and multi-scale-input methods (e.g. Noh et al.; Farabet et al.; Lin et al.; Chen et al., 2015b):** the paper's question is whether their costs are needed. Its own answer is a module with no pooling and no rescaling.

## Takeaways for practice

- (Own analysis) Dilation trades parameters for activations: receptive field grows with no new weights, but every layer runs at full map resolution with no pooling. Budget activation memory and compute for that; the paper gives no cost numbers.
- (Own analysis) Dilating layers after removed strides keeps pretrained weights usable at a denser output, avoiding retraining from scratch when a backbone must serve a higher-resolution head.
- (Own analysis) Identity initialization makes a bolted-on module a no-op at step zero, a cheap safeguard when extending a trained checkpoint; the paper reports random initialization failed.
- (Own analysis) Stage-wise training (front end, then context, joint only where it helps) splits one job into smaller checkpointable ones; on VOC-2012 the joint phase was unnecessary.
- (Own analysis) Audit components inherited from classification (intermediate padding, late pooling) before scaling a dense-prediction workload; removing them made the model simpler *and* more accurate (Sec. 4). Tie dilation depth to input resolution rather than treating it as a constant.
